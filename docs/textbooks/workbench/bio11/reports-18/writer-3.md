## Suspected errors and overgeneral claims (page, exact words, why, correction)
1. p. 1130 Q2 solution: "The only cation held outside at rest is Na⁺." Ca²⁺ is also higher outside (and is option (b) of the same question). Correction: "The main cation held outside at rest is Na⁺ ..." or "...the cation whose entry causes depolarisation is Na⁺".
2. p. 1130 Q1: "The ratio 3:2 is invariable across textbooks." Overgeneral flourish; harmless, could drop "across textbooks".
3. p. 1132 Q8 solution: "Multipolar neurons ... dominate the cerebral cortex and are motor neurons." Multipolar neurons are also interneurons; correction "...dominate the cerebral cortex; motor neurons are multipolar".
4. p. 1129 Traps table: "Grey matter is inside the cerebrum" is called a trap (false as a general statement), but the cerebrum also has deep grey matter (basal nuclei); row says "Brain: grey outside; spinal cord: grey inside". Correction: "cerebral cortex: grey outside".
5. p. 1120 E: "intrafusal muscle fibres (inner, sensory)": intrafusal fibres are modified muscle fibres with sensory endings wrapped around them, they are not themselves sensory. Correction: "intrafusal muscle fibres (inner, with sensory endings)". Also "This requires two interneurons" vs E.1's "At least two interneurons": use "at least two".
6. p. 1118 table: "Noradrenaline ... Sympathetic postganglionic": overgeneral (sympathetic fibres to sweat glands are cholinergic); also p. 1129 trap "Sympathetic; acetylcholine appears as a trap option" has the same exception. Correction: "most sympathetic postganglionic fibres".
7. p. 1122 F table: Meningitis "caused by bacteria or viruses" and F.2 "caused by infection (bacterial or viral)": also fungi and non-infectious causes; MS "relapsing-remitting": the commonest course, not the only one. Add "mostly".
8. p. 1127 Assertion-Reason approach: "If A is false, rule out (a), (b) and (c): only the last option can be right": true only with the standard fourth option "A false, R true" (the book itself notes some papers print "Both false" there, then A false and R false is also (d)).
9. p. 1124 G item 3: "Parasympathetic outflow: III, VII, IX, X" cranial only; the sacral parasympathetic outflow (S2 to S4) is not cranial, so correct for "cranial nerves" but "outflow" is incomplete. Suggest "Cranial parasympathetic outflow".
10. p. 1128 Archetype 4: "dorsal, posterior to the midbrain": the cerebellum lies behind the pons and medulla (below the occipital lobes), not directly behind the midbrain; harmless for the answer.
11. p. 1133 Q5 solution: "Damage to the small medulla stops breathing within seconds" and "pons ... does not contain the primary centre": fine in NCERT terms; the "within seconds" is a flourish.
12. p. 1124 H Distribution and p. 1125 verdicts ("Highest volume", "High-frequency"), p. 1125 Note, C intro "perennial": unquantified frequency claims, labelled as the book's estimate; reviewer may soften.
13. Name currency: "Brown-Sequard" (current usage Brown-Séquard); "norepinephrine" printed as the alternative name; none changed.
14. p. 1123 G item 1 and C practice 4 agree; p. 1116 "Anterolateral ... second-order neuron crosses to opposite side immediately" vs C.2 "within one or two spinal segments": consistent in meaning, "immediately" is loose.
15. p. 1122 F table is headed "The six disorders" while the intro names five (epilepsy is the sixth): not an error, noted.
