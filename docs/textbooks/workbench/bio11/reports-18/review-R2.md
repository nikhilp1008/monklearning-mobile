2. A, Method, paragraph after the refractory table (p.1112). Now: "Because Na+ channels are inactivated throughout this window, the impulse cannot overtake itself". Wrong: during depolarisation (part of this window) the Na+ channels are open, not inactivated; they inactivate at the peak. The book's own Example A.1 says "either open or already inactivated", and the table cell says "(all open then closed)". Same paragraph: "lasts roughly 1-2 ms, covering the depolarisation phase and the early part of repolarisation" is loose: with the table's 1 ms + 1 ms the absolute period runs from onset to the end of repolarisation (about 2 ms), then the relative period follows (the key point already says this). Fix [NEW]: "The absolute refractory period begins with the onset of depolarisation and lasts about 2 ms, to the end of repolarisation. Because the Na+ channels are open and then inactivated throughout this window, no new spike can start, so the impulse cannot overtake itself: this is the physical basis of unidirectional conduction along an axon." Table cell fix [NEW]: "Na+ channels open, then inactivated". Kind: biology error. Confidence certain (inactivated-throughout), likely (timing wording).

3. A, waveform table (p.1111). Now: Resting "Na-K pump working"; Restoration "Na-K pump restores gradients", "≈ 5-10 ms". The return from -90 to -70 mV in milliseconds is the closing of the voltage-gated K+ channels and the resting K+ leak; the pump restores the ion gradients much more slowly and only slightly changes the potential. The resting potential is set mainly by the K+ leak, with the pump keeping the gradients (the chapter says so, p.1080; the key point already says so, so the text and the KP now disagree). Fix [NEW]: Resting, key ion movement: "K+ leak sets the potential; Na-K pump keeps the gradients". Restoration: "K+ channels close; resting K+ leak returns the potential to -70 mV; Na-K pump later restores the gradients". Kind: biology error. Certain.

4. A, Introduction second paragraph (p.1111). Now: "gives only the resting ... and peak ... values, with no timings, no threshold value and no refractory-period logic." True: no timings and no numerical threshold. Fix [NEW]: "The chapter describes depolarisation, repolarisation and hyperpolarisation, but gives only the resting (about -70 mV) and peak (about +30 mV) values, with no timings, no numerical threshold and only a one-line mention of the refractory period. These are standard NEET questions." Also "These are standard NEET questions" is an unlabelled frequency claim: [NEW] "These are often asked in NEET." Kind: false never-covers claim. Certain.
