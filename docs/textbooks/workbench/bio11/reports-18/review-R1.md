2. "Brief overshoot (hyperpolarisation)" (p.1080, T01 part "Key processes", 3.2 step 3). "Overshoot" in standard usage is the part of the spike above 0 mV; the dip below rest is the undershoot or after-hyperpolarisation. A student who meets "overshoot" in an exam means +30 mV. Kind: nomenclature. Source: book. Confidence: certain.
   Fix [NEW]: "3. <b>Brief undershoot (hyperpolarisation).</b> Continued K<sup>+</sup> efflux can make the inside more negative than rest for an instant." (Fig 18.2 and KP already say hyperpolarisation without "overshoot": consistent.)

THALAMUS "almost all sensory and motor signals pass through it" (p.1089, T02 3.1 Thalamus) and HYPOTHALAMUS "the single point where electrical neural control hands over to chemical hormonal control" (p.1089, T02 3.1 Hypothalamus). Smell bypasses the thalamus; adrenal medulla (sympathetic) and the posterior pituitary are other hand-over points. Kind: overgeneral. Source: book. Confidence: certain.
   Fix [NEW] thalamus: "the great relay: most sensory signals (smell excepted) and many motor signals pass through it on the way to and from the cortex, so it acts as a central switchboard, filtering and forwarding information to the correct cortical area."
   Fix [NEW] hypothalamus: "...and it directs the pituitary gland, making it a main point where electrical neural control hands over to chemical hormonal control (the link you will trace further in the next chapter)."
