## Suspected book errors (page, quote, why, correction)
1. p. 995 H table "~65-70% of filtered Na+ and water (NCERT: 70-80%...)" vs p. 996 "about 65% by the usual physiology figure; NCERT gives 70-80%" vs p. 1006 Q2 "approximately 70-80 %" and p. 997 answer 4 "~70-80%": same chapter gives 65, 65-70 and 70-80 for the PCT. Sum check of the segment fractions (pp. 995-996, H.1): 65 + 25 + 5 + 3 = 98%, but 70 + 25 + 5 + 3 = 103% (> 100%), so the 65-70 range cannot hold at its upper end and NCERT's 70-80 cannot be combined with 25% for the thick ascending limb. Correction: say once that the segment fractions are the usual physiology figures (PCT about 65%), not NCERT's.
2. pp. 962/968 (not mine) and p. 996 "~99% of filtrate is reabsorbed -> about 1-1.8 L urine/day": NCERT 1-1.5 L; 1.8 L is 1% of 180 L. Also p. 1000 "~99 % ... (so ~1 % leaves as urine)". p. 997 answer 6 (180 - 1.5) over 180 x 100 = 99.17% recomputed: correct (178.5 / 180 = 0.99167).
3. p. 1006 Q3 "125 x 60 x 24 / 1000 ~ 180 L": 125 x 1440 = 180,000 mL = 180 L exactly (correct).  Q9 p. 1010 NFP 55 - (30 + 15) = +10 mmHg: correct; "if GHP fell below ~45 mmHg" correct (other pressures fixed).
4. p. 992 and 993 (G.2) desert mammals "4,000-8,000 mOsm/L", kangaroo rat "up to 8,000 mOsm/L in the inner medulla": measured maxima for kangaroo rats are about 5,000-6,000 mOsm/L urine; 8,000+ is nearer a hopping mouse. NCERT gives none of these (human 1,200 is NCERT). Correction: "about 5,000-6,000 for kangaroo rats; some desert rodents above 9,000", or mark as not from NCERT. Cheat sheet p. 998 "(up to 8,000 mOsm/L)" same.
5. p. 992 "Terrestrial animals ... Produce highly concentrated urine (hyperosmotic)": not general (adult frogs, and birds/reptiles/insects, make little or no hyperosmotic urine; they conserve water by uric acid). "ammonotelic not viable" overgeneral (some land crabs, isopods, snails lose ammonia as gas). "major challenge is water conservation, not water excess or deficit" is oddly worded (conservation answers a deficit).
6. p. 994 answer 3 and p. 992 "Insects ... uricotelic": aquatic insects are ammonotelic (Topic 02 table p. 954 lists "aquatic insects"). "all" overgeneral.
7. p. 996 H.1 and p. 997 "The segment that is impermeable to water is the thick ascending limb": among the five listed, the descending limb is impermeable to solutes; but the DCT (early) and collecting duct without ADH are also water-impermeable; "the" is true only with ADH absent for the last two. Correction: "the segment that is always impermeable to water".
8. p. 997 H practice 5 "Which segment secretes H+ ...? DCT and collecting duct": the book's own secretion table (p. 995) also lists the PCT (H+ via Na+/H+ exchange), the largest H+ secretor. Correction: add the PCT.
9. p. 998 cheat sheet "AQP2 absent -> no water permeability -> diabetes insipidus": only nephrogenic DI; central DI is lack of ADH (AQP2 present). Correction: "nephrogenic diabetes insipidus".
10. p. 1006 Q1 answer "(shells/membranes are impermeable)": egg shells are porous to gases; the point is that liquid and soluble waste cannot leave. Correction: "(the shell keeps soluble waste in the egg)". "Annelids are ... ureotelic" (earthworm: ureotelic when moist; NCERT lists earthworm nephridia only).
11. p. 1006 Q2 "by active transport": water and part of the solutes follow passively (the book says "passive water follows" on p. 995).
12. p. 999 / 1005 "Glomerular filtrate = plasma minus proteins", "Proteins are absent from filtrate and absent from urine": NCERT says the filtrate is "almost protein-free"; trace albumin is filtered and traces appear in urine. Correction: "almost protein-free", "normally negligible in urine".
13. p. 1005 trap "adult amphibians are ureotelic": aquatic adult amphibians (Xenopus) stay ammonotelic (Topic 02 p. 954 lists "aquatic amphibians" under ammonotelism). Correction: "adult land frogs".
14. p. 1004 "Every distractor pattern in these questions traces back to one of these"; p. 1000 "Five question shapes account for the vast majority" and "almost every year"; p. 1004 "the most reliably tested and most reliably missed" (p. 1011): unsupported superlatives/frequencies. I labelled them (below).
15. p. 1002 "Concentrated urine production in mammals is physically possible only because the loop of Henle ... ": "only" overgeneral (vasa recta and urea recycling also needed; the loop is necessary but not sufficient). Same in Q8 explanation, fine.
16. p. 1011 "the kidney only excretes it" (urea): the kidney also recycles urea (p. 995 UT-A1) and a little is made elsewhere; "only" overgeneral. p. 1011 "the descending limb ... nearly impermeable to salt; neither limb does both": fine.
17. p. 1007 Q5 prints "(d) Both false" (the standard fourth option is "A false, R true"); p. 1000 prose "four answer options: ... both false". The p. 1002 example prints "(d) A false, R true" correctly.
18. Spelling inconsistency only: "glucosuria" (p. 996 facts) vs "glycosuria"; "hilus" (Q7, NCERT "hilum"). Copied as printed.
