**Where I departed from the book's words** (all listed in the header comment, so each point stays true)
- **Circulation:** I never say all chordates have a closed circulation or a ventral heart. The four chordate features are the notochord, dorsal hollow nerve cord, gill slits and post-anal tail. Open means arthropods and most molluscs, closed means annelids and cephalopods. The book's Hemichordata "open", Chordata "closed" and annelid "first organised hearts" claims are not used.
- **Coelom origin:** schizocoelom is given for annelids and arthropods only (the book's Additional D also lists molluscs), and enterocoelom for echinoderms and hemichordates. Chordates are left out of coelom origin, and the book's Homo and Labeo example is dropped.
- **Cyclostomes:** they are vertebrates because of the cranium. I did not repeat "vertebral column" (hagfish has none) or "notochord persists".
- **Reptile and mammal wording:**
  - The reptile heart is "usually 3-chambered; 4-chambered in crocodiles", not "only reptiles with four".
  - Mammals are defined by hair, mammary glands and diaphragm, not viviparity.
  - The ear pinna is "in most mammals", and feathers are the exception-free bird feature, with no claim that every bird has pneumatic bones (penguins).
  - Omitted: the book's "7 cervical vertebrae" and "mostly viviparous" for Chondrichthyes.
- **Sponges and Ctenophora:** sponges are at the cellular grade, with the diploblastic and triploblastic labels starting at Cnidaria. I dropped the false trap clause "coelom terminology only from Cnidaria upward". The mesoglea is not called "non-cellular". Ctenophora stay "radial", as the chapter has it.
- **Other book claims not repeated:** "radula absent only in bivalves", "only freshwater sponge", the chordate "kidneys (pronephros to metanephros)" row and the "ciliated" ammocoete.
