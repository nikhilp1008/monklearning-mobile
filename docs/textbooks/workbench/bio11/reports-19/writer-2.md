## Suspected book errors (page, exact quote, why, correction)
1. p. 1156 (E, after the integrated response): "All four hormones act on liver, muscle, and adipose tissue to increase glucose output and reduce glucose utilization." Contradicts p. 1155 "muscle does not respond to glucagon"; muscle exports no glucose (G6Pase absent); glucagon is weakly lipolytic in humans. Correct: "Together they act mainly on the liver (glucose output) and on muscle and adipose tissue (reduced glucose uptake, lipolysis)."
2. p. 1157 (E practice 4): "Epinephrine causes glycogen breakdown in liver but not in muscle. Explain." False premise; the book's own Method (p. 1155) and answer say both break glycogen down. Correct the question: "...releases glucose from liver glycogen but not from muscle glycogen."
3. p. 1152 (C answer 2): "the parathyroids (which are themselves often damaged in CKD)". In CKD the parathyroids become hyperplastic and overactive. Correct: "which become enlarged and overactive".
4. p. 1149 (B answer 5): "The thirst centre is in the hypothalamus (lateral hypothalamic area)". Angiotensin II acts on the circumventricular organs (subfornical organ, OVLT); doubtful. Same page, Q3 asks for "the direct stimulus" (singular) but the answer gives two.
5. p. 1149 (B answer 3 and p. 1148 Step 4): "the two direct stimuli" / "the other direct stimulus": ACTH (and others) also act on the zona glomerulosa; overgeneral.
6. p. 1148/1149 (B.1): "ACE inhibitors block angiotensin-converting enzyme on the pulmonary endothelium": ACE is on endothelium in many tissues (kidney, vessels); "only" is not printed but the sense is narrow.
7. p. 1152 (D Step 1) vs p. 1154 (answer 3): Step 1 lists ADH among peptide hormones binding Gs-linked receptors; answer 3 puts "ADH (V1 receptor)" in the Gq pathway. Both true by receptor subtype (V2 Gs, V1 Gq) but the subtype is never said in Step 1. Also p. 1152 and Topic 07 p. 1140 list peptide hormones (insulin) as using second messengers; insulin's receptor is a tyrosine kinase (overgeneral "peptide and amine hormones ... generate a second messenger").
8. p. 1153 (D.1): "PKA ... translocates transcription factors to the nucleus": imprecise (PKA phosphorylates CREB; the catalytic subunit enters the nucleus). p. 1154 (answer 4): "multiplies the initial signal many thousand-fold": unquantified.
9. p. 1151 (C.1): "Calcitriol is required for intestinal calcium absorption." Overgeneral (it raises active absorption; some passive absorption persists).
10. p. 1156 (E answer 2, Q2): "which two hormones become the dominant glucose-raising hormones" (glucagon and cortisol): epinephrine and GH also act; "the two" is arguable.
11. p. 1155 (E): epinephrine in the liver "via β2-Gs-cAMP" only; hepatic glycogenolysis also uses α1 (Gq/Ca2+). Incomplete.
12. p. 1158 to 1159 (F.1): pyrogens "stimulate macrophages and endothelial cells to release PGE2": compressed; pyrogens make macrophages release IL-1, TNF, IL-6, which induce PGE2 in hypothalamic endothelium. Answer 5: "damaged cells and immune cells release arachidonic acid": it is released from membrane phospholipids by phospholipase A2 (p. 1159 answer 2 says so).
13. p. 1158 (F): "irreversibly acetylates COX-1 (and COX-2 at high doses)" vs p. 1159 "aspirin irreversibly inhibits both": inconsistent in degree.
14. p. 1160 (G): "tested in NEET and CBSE questions" and the "Examination pattern" column are unlabelled frequency-style claims; G omits Additional topic H. Not changed.
15. Exam wording: "NEET asks ..." and "Key actions tested in NEET" in B to F, "[NEET]" tags: no years, shares or counts on these pages, so nothing was labelled; each "NEET asks ..." is a statement of the book's own, unlabelled.
