## False "the chapter never covers X" (p.1144 intro, A "What the Chapter States")
1. A: "mentions the three axes in passing but does not trace any axis completely": Topic 09 item 1 traces the thyroid axis completely (TRH → TSH → T3/T4, negative feedback on hypothalamus and pituitary). True replacement: "traces the thyroid axis in outline and names the adrenal and gonadal axes, but gives none of their hormone chains in full." ("does not define trophic hormone" is true; Topic 09 even spells it "tropic".)
2. Intro: "the counter-regulatory hormones that govern blood glucose" cannot be developed: Topic 05 and Topic 04 already name glucagon, epinephrine, glucocorticoids as glucose-raising. Soften to "does not develop".
3. Intro: "The chapter gives you every gland, every hormone" (also Topic 01 "every gland, every hormone, every disorder"): false, e.g. no hCG, hPL, relaxin, leptin, ghrelin, GI hormones beyond four; also contradicts this very intro.
4. Intro: "the precise ... axes with their trophic hormone chain": Topic 09 gives the thyroid chain; fine only as "not fully".

## Suspected book errors / overgeneral claims (page, exact quote)
- p.1136: "Everything the chapter expects you to know is here" ; "Master the comparison tables and you cover almost the entire question surface" ; "one of the highest-yield chapters for NEET": unqualified.
- p.1138 (Topic 04): "hyperthyroidism → Graves’ disease (...) and can raise RBC count (polycythemia)": not NCERT; unsupported. Also "A lobular gland on the dorsal side of the heart, near the aorta" (thymus): thymus lies ventral/anterior to the heart and aorta (NCERT current: between lungs, behind sternum, ventral to aorta; older NCERT printed "dorsal"). Also "excess → osteitis fibrosa" is a clinical term not in NCERT (hyperparathyroidism).
- p.1139: "Insulin is the body’s only major glucose-lowering hormone": overgeneral (somatostatin, GLP-1/incretins also lower glucose). "persists through the first trimester": hCG maintains it; qualify as "typically".
- p.1140 (Topic 07): "Peptide/protein and amine hormones ... are water-soluble and cannot enter the cell": thyroid hormones are amino-acid derived yet cross the membrane (the next item says so); say "most".
- p.1141 (Topic 09): "every command hormone is matched by a feedback or antagonist signal": overgeneral (oxytocin and the LH surge are positive feedback). Same page spells "tropic hormone", A spells "trophic".
- p.1141 table: parathyroid disorder cell "osteitis / tetany" vs p.1138 "osteitis fibrosa"; heading says "excess / deficiency" order but thyroid cell lists "goitre, cretinism, myxoedema / Graves’" (Graves' is excess, the rest deficiency): fine, copied.
- p.1145: "All anterior pituitary hormones except prolactin and GH are trophic": GH also acts via IGF-1 (trophic-like); simplification, copied. MSH (pars intermedia) is listed with the anterior hormones on p.1137 and the cheat sheet but not in the six.
- p.1146: GnRH "pulses every 60-90 minutes in adult males": loosely 60-120; checked the sequence pGlu-His-Trp-Ser-Tyr-Gly-Leu-Arg-Pro-Gly-NH2 letter by letter: correct, 10 residues.
- Peptide lengths: TRH 3, CRH 41, ACTH 39, GnRH 10: all correct. Cortisol rhythm (low midnight, peak on waking): correct.
- p.1146 practice 3 asks "three" trophic hormones, answer lists four (TSH, ACTH, FSH, LH): copied.
- A.1 (p.1146) logic and tertiary naming correct. Practice 5 answer "the thyroid cannot respond to TSH" is one of several causes (also dysgenesis, enzyme defect).
- RAAS, cAMP, IP3, steroid receptors: nothing in my share disagrees with NCERT (RAAS steps are in B, not mine).
- Part 10/11 answers recomputed: no numbers; all keys consistent (P-3 Q-4 R-1 S-2 matches the stated pairs).
