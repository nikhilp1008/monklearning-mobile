## Flags, most serious first ([NEW] = our wording)

1. DEFECT, biology error, book. D, Practice answer 4 (iii), p. 1154. Says: "(iii) each cAMP activates many PKA molecules". Wrong direction: PKA is a tetramer that needs four cAMP bound (Step 4, p. 1153, says cAMP binds the regulatory subunits), so many cAMP are needed per PKA, not the reverse. Fix [NEW]: "(iii) the many cAMP molecules bind the regulatory subunits of PKA (four cAMP free the catalytic subunits of each PKA), so each active adenylyl cyclase switches on many PKA molecules;". Also the Introduction (p. 1152) "each activating one adenylyl cyclase, each producing many cAMP molecules" is fine. Certain.
2. E, Practice 4 (CUET), p. 1157. Says: "Epinephrine causes glycogen breakdown in liver but not in muscle. Explain." False premise; the book's own answer says both break glycogen down and only the liver exports glucose. Fix [NEW] question: "Epinephrine breaks down glycogen in both liver and muscle, but only liver glycogen raises blood glucose. Explain." (answer unchanged). Certain.
3. E, last paragraph of Method, p. 1156. Says: "All four hormones act on liver, muscle, and adipose tissue to increase glucose output and reduce glucose utilization." Contradicts the same topic ("muscle does not respond to glucagon"; muscle cannot export glucose). Fix [NEW]: "Together they act mainly on the liver (more glucose output) and on muscle and adipose tissue (less glucose uptake, more lipolysis)." Certain.
4. Topic 04, Thyroid disorders, p. 1138. Says: "hyperthyroidism → Graves' disease (exophthalmos, raised metabolism) and can raise RBC count (polycythemia)". Not NCERT, not a recognised feature to teach; NCERT only says thyroid hormones support RBC formation. Fix: delete " and can raise RBC count (polycythemia)". (R1's topic; same flag as writer 1.) Likely.
5. Topic 04, Thymus, p. 1138. Says: "A lobular gland on the dorsal side of the heart, near the aorta." Thymus lies behind the sternum between the lungs, ventral (in front) of the aorta and heart (NCERT wording). Fix [NEW]: "A lobular gland between the lungs, behind the sternum, on the ventral side of the aorta." Likely (R1's topic).
6. C, Practice answer 2, p. 1152. Says: "the parathyroids (which are themselves often damaged in CKD)". In CKD the parathyroids become enlarged and overactive (that is what "secondary hyperparathyroidism" means). Fix [NEW]: "Low calcium (and high phosphate) stimulates the parathyroids, which enlarge and become overactive (secondary hyperparathyroidism)". Certain. (Key point 14 already says it right.)

## Overall judgement
Transcription of my share is faithful word for word (no dropped or altered sentences, counts of blocks match the book apart from the writers' report slips). The book's Additional topics are mostly correct physiology (peptide lengths, RAAS, calcium, Gs/Gq, counter-regulatory hormones, prostaglandins all check out); the real errors are one reversed amplification statement (D answer 4), a false-premise question (E Practice 4), three overgeneral or self-contradicting sentences (E integrated response, C CKD parathyroids, the "every gland, every hormone" claim and the false "never traces an axis" claim), the A-R "(d) both false" slip that clashes with our own added options, and a heading that says three but lists four.
