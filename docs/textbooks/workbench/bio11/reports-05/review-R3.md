## Flags, most serious first

### 1. Solanaceae stamens called "fused" (serious)
- Where: topics-3-4.ts, Topic 04 (TOPICS[1]), Concept intuition, block 4 (book p. 315).
- Says now: "five fused epipetalous stamens on a berry-forming plant, it is Solanaceae".
- Wrong: Solanaceae stamens are free from one another (A<sub>5</sub>, no bracket; NCERT "stamens five, epipetalous"); only their adhesion to the corolla is a union. Every formula in the chapter writes A<sub>5</sub>, so this line contradicts the chapter and teaches a NEET trap backwards (fused stamens would be A<sub>(5)</sub>, as in the Asteraceae anthers).
- Fix: "five epipetalous stamens (free from one another, borne on the corolla) on a berry-forming plant, it is Solanaceae" [NEW]
- Kind: biology error. Source: book. Confidence: certain. NEW.

### 2. Micropyle placed "below" the hilum (serious)
- Where: topics-3-4.ts, Topic 03 (TOPICS[0]), "B. The Seed" table, row Micropyle (book p. 306).
- Says now: "small pore just below the hilum; route of water entry at germination".
- Wrong: NCERT's sentence, which NEET lifts, is "Above the hilum is a small pore called the micropyle." Up and down depend on how a seed is held, but the app should not contradict the NCERT line.
- Fix: "small pore just above the hilum (as NCERT puts it); route of water entry at germination" [NEW]. Optional, figure: in b11-05-fig-5.7 (A) the micropyle dot (moved by F1 to 155 degrees, below-left of the hilum) could sit just above the hilum instead.
- Kind: biology / NCERT contradiction. Source: book. Confidence: likely (NCERT wording certain). NEW.

### 3. "NEET note: NCERT's example of didynamous stamens is Salvia" is false, and Salvia is not didynamous (serious; earlier fix applied wrongly)
- Where: topics-1-2.ts, Topic 02 (TOPICS[1]), block 17 "Androecium: stamen structure & fusion" (book p. 297) and F1 item 14.
- Says now: "didynamous (2 long + 2 short, <i>Salvia</i>/<i>Ocimum</i>) ... <b>NEET note:</b> NCERT's example of didynamous stamens is <i>Salvia</i>."
- Wrong: NCERT never uses "didynamous"; it says "There may be a variation in the length of filaments within a flower, as in Salvia and mustard." Salvia has only two fertile stamens (the other two are staminodes or absent), so it is not didynamous; Ocimum (four stamens, two long and two short) is. The note added on R1 item 14 turns a book slip into a claimed NCERT fact.
- Fix: bullet: "didynamous (2 long + 2 short, <i>Ocimum</i>)" [NEW]; note: "<b>NEET note:</b> NCERT gives <i>Salvia</i> and mustard as its examples of filaments of different lengths within one flower; it does not use the word didynamous." [NEW]
- Kind: biology error / wrong attribution. Source: book (Salvia) + fixer (note). Confidence: likely. REPEAT (R1 14 applied wrongly; the writer had already noted "Salvia has two fertile stamens").

### 5. D Practice 5: a floral diagram asked to show the inferior ovary, and disc-floret petals called "perianth parts"
- Where: extras.ts, D (TOPICS[3]), Practice item 5 (book p. 338).

### 6. Fig 5.10 (Solanaceae floral diagram): stamens drawn opposite the petals
- Where: lib/diagrams/biology-11-05.ts, b11-05-fig-5.10 (book p. 311).

- Fix: rotate the stamen ring by 36 degrees so each stamen sits between two petals (still drawn touching the corolla, epipetalous); optionally join the sepal arcs and the petal arcs into continuous rings with notches. Caption unchanged.
- Kind: figure error. Source: book (copied faithfully). Confidence: likely. NEW.

### 9. "Each floret has its own calyx, corolla, stamens and ovary" (A practice 5, key point 21)
