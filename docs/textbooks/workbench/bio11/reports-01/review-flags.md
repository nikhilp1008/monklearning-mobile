## Biology errors, certain or likely (fix)
1. **Test-tube system "neither living nor non-living" (Topic 00: Concept intuition, Framework C steps 4 and 5, Ex 4(A), Practice 4, pitfall 3, cheat sheet row 4, Fig 1.1 verdict box, key point 0 #2; E: traps table row, Level 2 Q3, key point 7).** NCERT: isolated in-vitro metabolic reactions "are not living things but surely living reactions". Reword to "a living reaction, but the flask is not a living thing"; delete "(a surrogate)"; rewrite E Q3 as "(3) living reactions, but not living things", answer (3). Fig 1.1 follows the book: change it with the text. (R1 #1, R2 #2.)
2. **Monera cell wall "Peptidoglycan (with polysaccharide)" (D five-kingdom table p. 47, D.2 p. 50, Practice D1 answer p. 51).** Archaebacteria lack peptidoglycan; mycoplasma have no wall; NCERT: "non-cellulosic (polysaccharide + amino acid)". Cell: "Non-cellulosic (polysaccharide + amino acid); peptidoglycan in true bacteria, absent in archaebacteria; absent in mycoplasma". (R2 #1.)
3. **"Two genetically identical bacterial cells are by definition the 'same individual'" (B p. 40).** Wrong. [NEW] "Bacteria reproduce by binary fission, so a bacterial lineage is a set of clones and there is no interbreeding population or shared gene pool for the biological concept to test." (R2 #3.)
4. **Assertion-reason option (d) "both false" (E Archetype 5 p. 55, Q9 p. 61).** Standard format: "(d) A false, R true". Answers unchanged. (R2 #4.)
5. **"Order -ales, family -aceae" (E trap, Archetype 4).** Plants only. [NEW] "In plants, order -ales and family -aceae (animal orders have no fixed ending; animal families commonly end -idae)." (R2 #5.)
6. **Animal growth and "mutually exclusive" (E Archetype 2, Q7, Q11; pp. 53, 59–62).** NCERT: "most higher animals and plants"; cell division continues in some tissues. Three rewritten lines are in R2 #6. (R2 #6.)
7. **"The whole system was introduced by Linnaeus" (Topic 02 Concept intuition; cheat sheet "(Introduced by Linnaeus.)").** [NEW] "The system of ranked categories goes back to Linnaeus, who used kingdom, class, order, genus and species; family and phylum/division were added later." Cheat sheet: "(Begun by Linnaeus.)". (R1 #2.)
8. **Wrong "the chapter never covers" claims in the additional topics' introductions (A, B, C, D; shared p. 35).** (a) A: numerical taxonomy appears nowhere in Topics 00–02; Topic 01 names karyotaxonomy and chemotaxonomy, Topic 02 cytotaxonomy and chemotaxonomy. (b) A's second paragraph: NCERT Chapter 1 does not name them. (c) A quote omits "(groups)". (d) B: "never tells you … four variants … fails for two large groups" is wrong (Topics 01 and 02 already say it "gets fuzzy"/"breaks down" for asexual organisms and hybrids; the four are four species concepts; the table lists four failure groups). (e) C: "referenced in the chapter's opening vignette" not found. (f) D: "the five-kingdom table that the chapter sketches": there is no sketch. Replacement text for each is in R2 #7. (R2 #7.)
9. **Woese and the "six-kingdom" label (D title, p. 47, practice answers; key point 6).** Woese (with Fox, 1977) split the prokaryotes by 16S rRNA; the three domains came in 1990; the six-kingdom scheme is often credited to Woese in Indian material but is Cavalier-Smith's elsewhere. NCERT Class 11 does not name Woese. Reword as R2 #8. (R2 #8.)
10. **Consciousness "THE defining property" and the MCC bundle (Topic 00 and Fig 1.1 highlight).** [NEW] after the bundle paragraph: "NCERT calls consciousness the defining property of living organisms, and that is the answer to give when it is offered beside growth, reproduction or increase in mass. Metabolism and cellular organisation are also defining, which is why the book groups the three as 'MCC'." (R1 #3.)
11. **Cryptic-species example (B p. 40, key point 4):** *Xenopus laevis* group is not a cryptic example; the book's own Practice B4 uses the *Anopheles gambiae* complex. Replace. (R2 #9.)
12. **Bacterial species thresholds (B pp. 40–42, key point 4):** DNA–DNA hybridisation is a molecular, not "phenetic", approach; 97 % is the older 16S cut-off (about 98.65 % now). (R2 #11.)
13. **Cat named two ways (E Archetype 6 "Felis domestica" vs Topic 01 *Felis catus*).** Use *Felis catus*. (R2 #10.)
14. **Cytotaxonomy "Chromosomes are highly conserved … similar karyotypes are likely closely related" (A p. 36),** contradicted by the book's own Brassica example. (R2 #12.)
15. **Plant growth "throughout the body … a tree adds cells in its interior" (Topic 00).** Growth is at meristems. (R1 #15, possible.)
