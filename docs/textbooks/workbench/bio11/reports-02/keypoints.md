**Where I departed from the book or its chapter text** (all listed in the header comment):
- **Woese:** no year is given. The text has none, and the figure's 1977 is the rRNA year, not the domains'. Domains are a "higher rank, a refinement, not a replacement". I dropped "the five kingdoms still sit beneath them" because Monera is split. "Deepest split is Archaea vs Bacteria" is not stated as fact; Archaea closer to Eukarya is given as what the rRNA evidence shows.
- **Kingdom decision tree (Topic 00 shortcut):** the single-celled check goes beside the wall check. The `why` says diatoms and dinoflagellates are unicellular, walled and photosynthetic yet protists.
- **Chemoautotrophs:** they recycle nitrogen, iron and sulphur. The book's phosphorus is dropped.
- **Chlorophyll:** it is given as what separates cyanobacteria from ordinary heterotrophic bacteria. "Only cyanobacteria have chlorophyll" is not repeated.
- **Mycoplasma:** it has no wall at all and survives without oxygen (NCERT, per the errata). It is not called a facultative anaerobe and not placed among the peptidoglycan bacteria.
- **Protista:**
  - Chrysophytes are "diatoms and golden algae", not "diatoms and desmids".
  - The pellicle is not called unique to euglenoids.
  - Slime moulds "feed on decaying twigs and leaves" (not "saprophytic" and "engulfing" together).
  - "Slime moulds are single-celled" is not said.
  - Euglena is written as the plant-like mixotroph.
  - Euglena's missing wall rules out Plantae, together with the unicellular body, the flagellum and mixotrophy. The book's "single feature" wording is dropped.
- **Animalia wall:** "only animal cells lack a wall" is replaced. Animal cells have no wall, but *Mycoplasma* and most protozoans also lack one, so no wall alone does not prove Animalia.
- **Fungi:** the "*Neurospora*, Drosophila of the plant kingdom" line is not used, and the Phycomycetes' sexual spore is not named. - **Albugo:** it is a phycomycete (NCERT and the chapter). The header notes that modern schemes put it among the oomycetes.
