## Suspected book errors (page, exact quote, why, correction, confidence)
Dates and numbers (copied; NCERT value and my confidence given):
1. p. 316 table "Australopithecus ~3–4 mya" (also Q3 "~3–4 mya", p. 320): NCERT gives about 2 mya for Australopithecus. The book's 3-4 mya is closer to current work (about 4 to 2 mya). Book differs from NCERT; high confidence it is not wrong for current science.
2. p. 316 table "Homo sapiens ~75,000 ya → present"; p. 318 "arose in Africa and, during the last ice age (roughly 75,000–10,000 years ago), migrated": 75,000 to 10,000 years is NCERT's value; current work dates the origin of H. sapiens to about 300,000 years ago (Jebel Irhoud) and the spread out of Africa to about 70,000-60,000 years ago. The sentence "arose in Africa and, during the last ice age ... migrated" is fine; the table's "~75,000 ya" as the start is not current. High.
3. p. 316 "Homo neanderthalensis 100,000–40,000 ya": NCERT value; current work about 400,000 to 40,000 ya. High.
4. p. 316 "Homo erectus ~1.5 mya, ~900 cc": matches NCERT. Current work (about 1.9 mya to 110,000 ya) differs; brain 600-1200 cc. Low concern. "Homo habilis ~2 mya, 650–800 cc" match NCERT; "probably did not eat meat" is NCERT's wording, current work says habilis scavenged and ate meat (medium).
5. p. 318 "cave art appeared around 18,000 years ago" (also p. 316 table "agriculture ~10,000 ya"): 18,000 and 10,000 are NCERT's values. Dated cave art is far older (over 40,000 years: Sulawesi, Spain); agriculture about 10,000 is fine. High for cave art.
6. p. 323, 324, 326, 327 "extinction of the dinosaurs about 65 million years ago" (also MCQ 3, cheat sheet): NCERT says 65; current work about 66. Low-medium; the brief names it.
7. p. 324 and p. 330 "~2000 mya First cellular (single-celled) life appears": NCERT states 2000 mya, but the earliest cellular life is about 3.5 billion years old (stromatolites, microfossils), after Earth at about 4.5 bya. Wrong by about 1.5 billion years in current science. Appears in the timeline table, cheat sheet and the Figure 6.4 axis. High.
8. p. 324 "~350 mya Jawless fishes evolve" and "~350 mya Lobefins move onto land → first amphibians": jawless fishes date to about 500 mya (Cambrian/Ordovician); the lobefin-to-amphibian transition is about 375 to 365 mya. The book gives jawless fishes and amphibians the same date, which also breaks the "firsts you must order" logic. NCERT's own timeline gives ~350 for both. High on jawless fishes.
9. p. 324 Palaeozoic row "Ferns (pteridophytes)": ferns and lycophytes dominate the Carboniferous only; the early Palaeozoic had algae and bryophytes. Low. p. 325 "giant tree-ferns": Carboniferous coal swamps were mainly giant club mosses and horsetails (also pteridophytes, so the group is right); low.
