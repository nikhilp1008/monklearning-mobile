## Suspected book errors (page, text, why, correction, confidence)
1. p. 737 practice A.6 and its answer: "If beta diversity between two habitats is 3.0": with the book's own β = γ/ᾱ, two habitats give 1 ≤ β ≤ 2 (β = 2 only if they share no species). A β of 3.0 needs at least 3 habitats. Say "among three or more habitats" (or use 1.8 as in A.3). Confidence high.
2. p. 736 A.2 answer: "Community A has higher Simpson's Index (0.90 vs 0.17)". On p. 734 the book defines Simpson's Index as Σp² (dominance, high = LOW diversity); 0.90 and 0.17 are Simpson's Index of Diversity D. Write "Simpson's Index of Diversity". Confidence high (naming only; the values are right).
3. p. 743 to 746 IUCN list omits "Extinct in the Wild (EW)" (between EX and CR; the brief's own list names it) and the official order EX, EW, CR, EN, VU, NT, LC, DD, NE; practice 2 and D.3 ("remaining ~68% among NT, LC, EX, DD, NE") would then include EW. The thresholds are the IUCN criterion A2 to A4 values (≥ 80, ≥ 50, ≥ 30 per cent over 10 years or three generations, whichever is longer; A1 is ≥ 90, 70, 50) and "fewer than 50 mature individuals" is criterion D of CR; the book's "or" for "10 years or three generations" should be "whichever is longer". Confidence high on EW, medium on the wording of thresholds.
4. p. 745 D.3: "approximately 32% of all amphibian species fall into CR, EN, or VU" vs the p. 744 text "assessed amphibian species": the share is of assessed species (data-deficient ones excluded in IUCN's usual figure). Medium.
5. p. 741 C.4 and p. 742: item (R) "Indian hotspot with more than 1,500 endemic plants" matches "(i) Western Ghats–Sri Lanka" only by elimination: every hotspot has at least 1,500 endemic vascular plants by definition (p. 723), and practice C.3 itself gives Indo-Burma "~7,000 endemic plants". Reword (R) to "Indian hotspot that also includes Sri Lanka". Confidence high.
6. p. 741 "Western Ghats: over 1,500 endemic flowering plant species" and C.4 (R), practice 3: true but understated (the hotspot has about 3,000 endemic vascular plants; 1,500 is the qualifying threshold); Himalaya about 3,000, Indo-Burma about 7,000. Medium (recollection of Conservation International's figures). The "(NCERT-aligned)" tag on both lists: NCERT names Eichhornia, Parthenium, Lantana, Clarias; Prosopis juliflora, Eucalyptus, the endemic counts and Hydrophylax malabaricus are not NCERT as I recall. Low to medium.
7. p. 741 C.1: the step headed "Endemic to India:" lists (i) and (iii) and then says (iii) is not endemic: the heading reads wrong. Say "Native:" or split. Medium-low; the answer line is right.
8. p. 738 B.4 and p. 740 practice 6: "the species-area relationship flattens at very large scales as the species pool is exhausted" (B.4 reason (i)) contradicts the same addendum (continental z is steeper, 0.6 to 1.2); and "rectangular hyperbola that saturates" (practice 6, also p. 715 and the pitfall p. 720) is not true of S = CA^z, a power law with no plateau (z < 1 gives a concave curve that keeps rising; z = 0.6 to 1.2 includes z ≥ 1, convex or linear). Keep NCERT's "rectangular hyperbola" wording (NCERT says it) and add: "on ordinary axes a power law curves upward, rising more slowly at larger areas; it does not reach a plateau". Medium-high. Also practice B.4: 10^6 to 10^9 ha for "continental" is 10^4 to 10^7 km^2 (a region to Asia), loose; low.
9. p. 734 Simpson: the name "Simpson's Index" is used for both Σp² and 1 − Σp² in the literature; fine as defined, but the shared "Simpson's Index (dominance)" wording differs from many NCERT-level sources. Low.
10. p. 736 A.3: "species composition changes about 1.8 times over the region" misreads Whittaker's beta (the regional pool is 1.8 times the mean local richness, i.e. 1.8 effective communities); wording only. Low.
11. p. 735 A.1: the four products sum to −1.209 (the book says Σ ≈ −1.21 and "−(−1.208)"); exact sum is −1.2080, so the rounded table entries are the source. No change needed. Typical Shannon "1.5–3.5" (p. 734) is a rule of thumb (it can exceed 4 in very rich communities); low.
12. Superlatives and overgeneral lines (copied): p. 734 "exactly what a top-scoring student still needs"; "every year" (labelled); p. 741 "Endemic species ... have evolved in isolation and survive only within that range" (relict, palaeo-endemics, and recently evolved ones differ; "survive only within" is the definition); "never exotic" (true within the home range as the book restricts it); "the highest-priority conservation targets" (p. 741) and "the highest conservation priority" (p. 743 answer 2): priority also depends on threat (the chapter's hotspot rule is endemism AND threat, p. 723); "any threat to that single area removes the entire global population" (p. 743 answer 6): overstated, a local threat removes the population only if it covers the whole range. Parthenium: "accidentally introduced through contaminated grain shipments" (p. 743, p. 741 "wheat imports"): widely stated for wheat imports (1950s PL-480); "grain" and "wheat" are used interchangeably; NCERT gives no origin or route. Low.
13. p. 745 practice D.1 (ii): a frog "last seen 40 years ago ... found despite searches" is labelled Extinct; IUCN requires exhaustive surveys over the right time frame, otherwise it stays CR (possibly extinct). The book says "exhaustive searches" in the step, "searches" in the stem. Low.
14. p. 746 practice D.6: compares extinctions in a 50-ha fragment (local extirpation) with the global background rate; the arithmetic is right (5/20 = 0.25 per year = 25 per century; 200 x 10^-6 = 2 x 10^-4 per year = 0.02 per century; 25/0.02 = 1,250). The comparison is not like for like; a note, not a number fix. Low.
15. p. 744 "Current rate: 100–1000 species per million species per year" is right (100 to 1000 times 1 E/MSY); NCERT's "100 to 1000 times" and background "1 per million species per year": as printed.
