## Suspected book errors and overgeneral claims (page, exact quote, why, correction, confidence)
1. p. 750 table: "The in-situ/ex-situ boundary is tested relentlessly." Exam superlative, unlabelled; replace "is tested in many papers" or drop. High.
2. p. 750 table: "These appear every 2–3 years." Unlabelled frequency; covered by the label above the table; not verifiable. Medium.
3. p. 750 table: "(HOAC) is a direct-recall fixture" and "appear in multiple sessions": exam-frequency claims, labelled once by the paragraph above the table. Low.
4. p. 753: "First memorise the fixed pairs that never change" (and "never change" for four pairs): overgeneral; Column II wordings vary between papers (for instance "narrowly utilitarian" is paired with "obvious reasons", not "direct economic benefits", in the book's own example on the same page). Correction: "pairs that recur". Medium-high.
5. p. 751 vs p. 753/758: "Animals >70 % of all species" and "Animals make up more than 70 %" against "about 70 %" in the example, Figure 13.4 (animals 70 %) and Figure 13.5 (insects 49 + other animals 21 = 70). Inconsistent qualifier: >70 vs about 70; with 49 % (= 0.70 × 0.70) "more than 70 %" would give a little over 49 %. Correction: "about 70 %". Confidence high. Recomputation: 0.70 × 0.70 = 0.49; 49 + 21 = 70; 22 + 8 = 30; 70 + 22 + 8 = 100: all agree with p. 729/730 text (Figure 13.4: animals 70, plants 22, other 8; Figure 13.5: insects 49, other animals 21, plants incl. algae and fungi 22, other 8). The "plants" wedge includes algae and fungi in Figure 13.5, so "fungi exceed all vertebrates" and "plants 22 %" overlap in labelling (not an error, worth a caption note).
6. p. 754 trap: "Exotic (non-native) species reduce biodiversity by outcompeting natives" and "Exotic species usually increase biodiversity → FALSE": overgeneral. Many introduced species (crops, ornamentals) do not displace natives; invasive alien species do. Correction: "Invasive exotic species reduce biodiversity...". Also title "Exotic = dangerous" overgeneral. High.
7. p. 754 trap: "The correct recent extinction is the passenger pigeon (North America, 1914)". The passenger pigeon (Ectopistes migratorius, last bird Martha died 1914) is correct as history but is not in NCERT's list of named extinct species (Dodo, Quagga, Thylacine, Steller's sea cow, three tiger subspecies). The trap's premise (what a question lists as extinct) cannot be checked. Confidence: fact high, NCERT absence medium-high. Italicise-free as printed.
8. p. 755 trap: "Only off-site storage (seed bank, zoo, cryopreservation) is ex situ." Overgeneral list: botanical gardens, safari parks, gene banks and in vitro collections are also ex situ (the book's own Study Tips on p. 758 and archetype 2 on p. 752 add botanical garden and safari park). Correction: "Off-site storage and display (seed bank, zoo, botanical garden, safari park, cryopreservation) is ex situ." High.
9. p. 755 trap: "34 vs 36 hotspots ... NCERT (older editions) says 34; Conservation International now says 36." Conservation International recognises 36 hotspots (high); the current NCERT Class 12 text still says 34 (I believe; medium), so "older editions" implies a change that may not exist. No year added. Same on p. 758 tip 2 ("current papers occasionally signal the updated figure of 36"): unverifiable, labelled-exam claim. Brief expects three Indian hotspots (Western Ghats and Sri Lanka, Himalaya, Indo-Burma); India is also in Sundaland (Nicobar), which some counts include (4 in India under the 36 count). Medium.
10. p. 755 trap: "global warming ... is not one of the four named causes in the textbook" true for NCERT; fine. "NCERT's Evil Quartet ... mnemonic HOAC": fine.
11. p. 755 "A Graded Practice Set": "All answers have been checked against NCERT." Not true of Q6 (the 70 %/1,500 plant hotspot criteria are Myers/Conservation International criteria; NCERT Class 12 does not print them; Q6 also omits the 1,500 endemic plant condition so "very high endemism" is not the full criterion) and Q11 (biosphere-reserve core/buffer/transition zonation is not in the NCERT Class 12 chapter, as I recall; medium) and Q8 (β-diversity explanation is the book's own). Correction: drop or soften the claim. Medium-high.
12. p. 756 Q4: option (d) printed "Both false" (not the standard "A false, R true"): written as standard (exception 7). Key (c) is right: A true, R false. High.
13. p. 757 Q8: option (a) "continental areas have more habitat types than a park" is also a true contributor to a steeper slope; keyed (b) as "the mechanistic explanation". Options (a), (c), (d) get no nudge (the book explains none of them; see below). z = 0.95 lies in the 0.6–1.2 continental range and 0.15 in 0.1–0.2: fine. (c) "always larger by convention" is correctly false. Medium.
14. p. 757 Q9: "(c) and (d) invert the true order": (d) molluscs are the second-largest animal group, not an inversion; (a) gets no explanation. Low-medium. The key (b) is correct (NCERT: fungi outnumber the combined fish, amphibians, reptiles and mammals; book says "all vertebrate species combined", the same, though birds are also counted if "all vertebrates" is read strictly; the Figure 13.5 caption has the same wording; NCERT lists fishes, amphibians, reptiles, birds and mammals, high confidence it is fine).
15. p. 757/758 Q10: "(c) Release of about 20 % of the Earth's oxygen by the Amazon rainforest" printed as fact, no caveat. NCERT prints "20 per cent" but it is a popular overstatement (the forest's net oxygen contribution is close to zero because respiration and decay use most of what photosynthesis releases; the commonly quoted "20 %" is of gross production). The book words it without a caveat; recommend "NCERT states about 20 %, which is a popular overstatement". High on the science, medium on exact NCERT wording.
16. p. 752 A-R example: R "less year-to-year variation in total biomass" almost restates A (stability), so "R explains A" is true but circular; as a teaching example the options aid should say R is the measured observation of stability. Low.
17. p. 756 Q1 "IUCN Red List (2004)" and "Q1 ... 784": copied; 338 + 359 + 87 = 784 recomputed, agrees. The "2004" is NCERT's dated baseline; no current figure added. The Red List count has risen since. High.
18. p. 754 archetype 6: z = 0.25 is outside the 0.1 to 0.2 band the book gives for a region and below the 0.6 to 1.2 continental band; the book hedges "or just above it for a regional study" (an unstated extension of the band). Suggest saying the band is "about 0.1 to 0.2, sometimes a little higher". Log base is 10 (10^1.40, 10^1.90). Medium.
19. p. 751 archetype 1: example option "(a) below species level" etc. is fine. p. 751 "Approach: ... The distractor will invert a qualifier": "will" overgeneral. Low.
20. p. 758 tip 1: "NEET's most reliable trap" superlative, unlabelled. Replace "a frequent trap". High.
21. p. 747 notation: the book uses N both for the second sample size and (as N_total) for the population (Lincoln–Petersen N = M × C over R with C = second sample): formula and numbers fit, but the double use of N invites error; consider C (or n) for the second sample. Low-medium. p. 749 E.4 "R is at least 5–10" is a rule of thumb, not a theorem. Low.
