## Suspected book errors and overgeneral lines (page, exact words, why, fix, confidence)
1. p. 715 and 720/721 (Pitfall 2) "tracing a rectangular hyperbola", "richness rises with area, then flattens; it is a saturating curve", "plateau": S = C·A^z (z < 1) is a power law, a straight line on log–log axes and it does NOT level off; a rectangular hyperbola/saturating curve is a different function. The book's own Ex 3 predicts 79 species with no limit. NCERT uses "rectangular hyperbola" too. Fix: say the curve is a power law that rises ever more slowly (concave, no true plateau on a single region). Confidence 85%. Propagates to: Section 1 limiting conditions, Section 2, Pitfall 2, Figure 13.1 caption and its "plateau" label.
2. p. 716 "z and C are dimensionless fitted constants": C is not dimensionless, S = C·A^z with S a count means C carries area^(−z) (e.g. ha^(−0.25) in Ex 3). Fix: "z is dimensionless; C has units of area^(−z)". Confidence 85%. Also "C = Y-intercept constant" is true only on the log–log plot (the intercept there is log C). 70%.
3. p. 716, p. 720 Practice 2/answer 2, p. 721 cheat sheet: causes "in order of importance", "order of harm", "habitat first, always", "Over-exploitation > Alien species > Co-extinction": NCERT calls habitat loss the most important cause but ranks no other; the ranking of the other three is the book's. Fix: say only that habitat loss and fragmentation is generally ranked first (IUCN threat data); drop the ordering of the rest or mark it as the book's. Confidence 70%. "always" overgeneral.
4. p. 718 "the Amazon alone is estimated to produce ~20% of atmospheric oxygen via photosynthesis" (also copies NCERT): popular overstatement; the forest's respiration and decay use up almost all it makes, net contribution to atmospheric oxygen is close to zero. The book prints no caveat. Confidence 90%.
5. p. 718 Ex 4 "the highest fraction among the major groups" for amphibians (32%): true among birds, mammals, amphibians, gymnosperms as listed, but IUCN lists cycads (gymnosperms) far higher and "major groups" is undefined.
