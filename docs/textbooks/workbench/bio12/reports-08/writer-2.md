## Suspected book errors (page, exact words, why, correction, confidence)
1. p. 452 MCQ 2: "(d) are food/household microbes (curd, bread, cheese) from the previous subtopic." The previous topic is Topic 04 (biogas); Lactobacillus, Saccharomyces and Propionibacterium are in Topic 01. Wrote "from Topic 01". High.
2. p. 437, 438, 441-443 (Topic 04 throughout): "Methanogens are anaerobic bacteria". Methanogens are archaea (Methanobacterium is in Archaea, phylum Euryarchaeota); NCERT also says "bacteria". Copy; correction "anaerobic archaea (often called archaebacteria in NCERT)". High on the fact, medium on whether to change given NCERT wording. Same wording in Table 4.1, Q1 model answer, MCQ 1 option (b), cheat sheet.
3. p. 437, 438, 441: methanogens "grow on cellulosic material". NCERT says so, but cellulose is broken down by other (cellulolytic) bacteria in the rumen/digester; methanogens use the products (H2, CO2, acetate). The book's "help break down cellulose" (p. 438, 441 role in cattle) also credits methanogens with cellulose digestion. Medium confidence; keep the NCERT wording and add the nuance beside it.
4. p. 438 Table 4.1 and p. 441 Q1, p. 443: biogas = "methane + carbon dioxide + hydrogen" (NCERT) while p. 431 step 6 and p. 435 MCQ 4 list "methane, hydrogen sulphide, and carbon dioxide": the two lists differ (the book never gives H2S in Topic 04 or H2 in Topic 03). Both are real components (H2S as a trace); copy, report the inconsistency. High.
5. p. 441 Q2: "(a)/(b) are minor components": CO2 is typically about 30 to 40 per cent of biogas, not a "minor" gas next to 60 per cent methane (Fig 8.6 on p. 440 prints "about 60% CH4"); H2S is a trace. Say "smaller components". Medium.
6. p. 452 Practice 5 answer and p. 447 step 5: Bt "species-specific ... bees (different gut chemistry, non-target) are spared". The Cry toxin acts through specific receptors on gut cells of susceptible insect orders (and many insect guts, including some non-targets, are alkaline), so the specificity is receptor binding, not only gut pH; "bees have different gut chemistry" is an unsupported simplification. Medium. Also "the chemical pesticide acts ... on the nervous system of most insects" is general (many do), not all; low.
7. p. 444 limiting conditions: "A field with some ladybird beetles never has an aphid plague" ("never"): overgeneral; predators reduce but do not exclude outbreaks. High.
8. p. 445 "Rhizobium fixes nitrogen only in symbiosis with the root nodules of legumes; put it with wheat and nothing happens": NCERT-level truth, but Rhizobium also nodulates Parasponia (a non-legume) and some strains fix N in culture under special conditions; also p. 447 "Rhizobium ... lives symbiotically inside root nodules of leguminous plants". Low-medium.
9. p. 445 "Biocontrol agents must be species-specific" / "A good agent kills only the target pest" / "A 'broad-spectrum' biological killer would be no better than a chemical": overgeneral (predators such as ladybirds and dragonflies, listed in Table 5.1, are not species-specific). Medium.
10. p. 446 Table 5.1: Trichoderma "(free-living, root ecosystems)" is NCERT wording; fine. Table 5.1 lists "Baculovirus (Nucleopolyhedrovirus)" for "Insects and other arthropods": NCERT. OK. p. 446 "Cyanobacteria ... Free-living (and some symbiotic)": fine.
11. p. 446 "The gene coding this toxin is cry; it is the same gene engineered into Bt cotton": the bacterium has several cry genes (cryIAc, cryIIAb, cryIAb in NCERT Ch. 12); "the gene" simplifies. Low-medium.
12. p. 430 comparison (p. 433) "Effect on BOD: Little" for primary treatment: primary settling typically removes a fraction (about 25 to 40 per cent) of BOD; "little" is loose. Low. p. 430 "The most common slip", "the single most tested distinction", p. 435 "Almost every sewage MCQ is a fragment of this spine", p. 438/443 "Examiners love this causal chain", "frequently tested", p. 445-446 "Examiners constantly swap these", p. 444 "One of the single most NEET-loaded subtopics in the entire chapter", p. 437 "dependable scorer", "high marks-per-word": unsupported superlatives and frequency claims; copied, labelled where they are frequencies (see above). 
13. p. 433 Q2 "(c)/(d) are distractors" and p. 441 "(a)/(b) are minor": slashes between option letters left as printed (not flagged).
14. p. 452 MCQ 2 stem "The most appropriate group of biocontrol agents": "most appropriate" is loose (the answer is the only group made wholly of biocontrol agents). Low.
15. p. 453 Pitfall 4 and 5.2: "Trichoderma fights fungi, not insects": fine for the standard NCERT role (some strains have minor insecticidal effects: ignore). p. 447 step 5 "Because activation and binding are specific, Bt kills the caterpillars but leaves other insects unharmed": overgeneral ("all"): some Bt strains (cry classes) target beetles and flies. Medium.
