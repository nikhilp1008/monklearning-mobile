# Review R1: Biology 12 Chapter 8, Topics 00 to 05, figures, key points (topic indexes 0 to 5)

Files read in full: content/textbooks/bio-12-08/topics-0-2.ts, topics-3-5.ts, lib/diagrams/biology-12-08.ts (source and every label), lib/revise/keypoints-biology-12-08.ts (indexes 0 to 5 checked line by line; 6 to 12 are the other reviewer's). Nothing edited. [NEW] = our wording.

## Flags, most serious first

1. Methanogens called "bacteria" (Topic 04, book pp. 437, 438, 441 to 443). Where: Topic 04 Concept intuition "These are bacteria that grow anaerobically"; Table 4.1 "Nature of methanogens"; CBSE Q1 model answer "anaerobic bacteria"; MCQ 1 option (b) "anaerobic bacteria that produce methane" (marked correct); cheat sheet "anaerobic bacteria (e.g. Methanobacterium)". Wrong: methanogens (Methanobacterium) are archaea (NCERT says "bacteria"). Fix [NEW]: "These are anaerobic microbes (archaea; NCERT calls them bacteria)". MCQ 1 option (b) [NEW]: "anaerobic microbes (archaea) that produce methane"; options (a) "aerobic bacteria that produce oxygen" stays wrong. Kind biology/nomenclature. Source book (NCERT wording). Certain on fact; the key points already say archaea, so chapter and key point disagree.
2. Monascus purpureus "(yeast)" (Topic 02 Table 2.4; "How statins lower cholesterol" step 1 "the yeast Monascus purpureus"). Wrong: a filamentous mould (red yeast rice mould); the chapter's own Pitfall 1 calls Aspergillus and Monascus "the two fungi". Fix [NEW]: Table 2.4 "<i>Monascus purpureus</i> (fungus, a mould)"; step 1 "produced by the mould <i>Monascus purpureus</i>". Biology error, source book/NCERT. Certain. Key point already says fungus.
3. Topic 02 MCQ 4 "Antibiotics are NOT effective against" is not a clean single-answer item. Options "bacteria" and "some bacterial infections of the lung" and "certain fungi" can each be read as true or false (resistant bacteria; an antibacterial drug does not act on fungi), and the nudge "(a), (b), (d) are all organisms against which antibiotics can act" is false for (b). Fix [NEW] stem: "Antibiotics are generally NOT effective against:" with options "bacteria that are sensitive to them", "viruses causing the common cold", "all kinds of bacteria alike", "the spores of no organism"... simplest: keep (c) and replace the other three with "Gram-positive bacteria", "Gram-negative bacteria (for a broad-spectrum antibiotic)", "sensitive Streptococcus", nudge "(a), (b), (d) are bacteria that antibiotics can act on". Kind wrong/ambiguous MCQ. Source book. Likely. The key point already avoids it.
4. Topic 00 "six NCERT sections ... lists five". Writer proposes "five"; I disagree. NCERT Chapter 8 has six sections (8.1 Household, 8.2 Industrial, 8.3 Sewage, 8.4 Biogas, 8.5 Biocontrol agents, 8.6 Biofertilisers), and the book's last item "Microbes as Biocontrol Agents and Biofertilisers" is two of them. "Six" is true; only the list reads as five. Fix [NEW]: "It consolidates all six NCERT sections in the order they appear in the textbook: Household Products, Industrial Products, Sewage Treatment, Production of Biogas, and Microbes as Biocontrol Agents and Biofertilisers (the last two NCERT sections, treated together in one topic)." Key point index 0 says "That is five topics ... (the note says “six” but lists five)": change to "That is five topics, covering the six NCERT sections (biocontrol and biofertilisers share one topic)". Certain.
5. Overgeneral "never" (Topic 05 Concept intuition, p. 444): "A field with some ladybird beetles never has an aphid plague". Wrong: predators reduce but do not exclude outbreaks. Fix [NEW]: "A field with ladybird beetles usually keeps aphids at a harmless level, because the beetles eat the aphids down and then, lacking food, thin out themselves." Certain. (Key points already avoid it.)
6. Species-specificity overstated (Topic 05): (a) Limiting conditions "Biocontrol agents must be species-specific. A good agent kills only the target pest ... A 'broad-spectrum' biological killer would be no better than a chemical"; ladybirds and dragonflies (Table 5.1) are not species-specific. Fix [NEW]: "Biocontrol agents should be as selective as possible: a good agent controls the target pest and spares non-target organisms ... baculoviruses are prized for their narrow host range." (b) Table 5.1 Bt row "sparing other insects"; Bt proc step 5 "Non-target insects are spared ... leaves other insects unharmed"; CBSE Q3 "insects lacking the right (alkaline) gut conditions and binding sites are unaffected"; practice 5 "bees (different gut chemistry, non-target) are spared"; Fig 8.7 note "the toxin is specific to the target insects". Wrong: Cry toxins bind specific gut receptors and each strain acts on a limited group of insects (mainly moth/butterfly larvae, some strains beetles or flies); alkaline gut alone does not explain it (many insect guts, bees' included, are not the reason). Fix [NEW] step 5: "Because activation and binding need the right receptors, a given Bt toxin kills only a limited group of insects (mainly caterpillars) and leaves most other insects, such as bees, unharmed." Practice 5 answer [NEW]: "Bt toxin is a protoxin activated in the alkaline gut of the susceptible larvae and binds receptors only on their gut cells, so it acts on a narrow group (caterpillars) and bees are spared; the chemical pesticide acts broad-spectrum on the nervous system of many insects, so it killed the bees as well." Figure note [NEW]: "Non-target insects (e.g. a bee) are largely unharmed: the toxin acts on a limited group of insects". Kind overgeneral. Source book (Fig 8.7 note printed on p. 448). Likely.
7. Topic 04 CUET Q2 solution: "(a)/(b) are minor components". CO2 is about 30 to 40 per cent, H2S a trace. Fix [NEW]: "(a) and (b) are present but are not the predominant gas." Likely.
8. Topic 04 cattle/rumen wording. (a) Think-about-it "a walking, four-stomached fermentation tank" contradicts "rumen (a part of the stomach)": ruminants have one stomach of four chambers. Fix [NEW]: "a walking fermentation tank with a four-chambered stomach". (b) "eating organic matter and breathing out methane": methanogens do not breathe; [NEW] "using the hydrogen and carbon dioxide and releasing methane". (c) Concept, Table 4.1 "Role in cattle: help break down cellulose", Q1, cheat sheet "aid cellulose digestion": cellulose is digested by other rumen microbes (cellulolytic bacteria, protozoa, fungi); methanogens use the H2 and CO2 they release. Keep NCERT wording and add [NEW]: "(the cellulose is broken down mainly by other rumen microbes; methanogens use the H<sub>2</sub> and CO<sub>2</sub> released and keep the rumen working well)". Source book. Medium. The key point (index 4) already carries this nuance; the chapter does not.
9. Penicillin claims (Topic 02 Table 2.2 and CBSE Q1 and cheat sheet): "Impact: Cured once-deadly diseases (plague, whooping cough, diphtheria, leprosy)" attached to penicillin; "First antibiotic: Penicillin". Penicillin is not the drug for plague or leprosy; NCERT's sentence is about antibiotics in general. Fix [NEW]: "Impact: antibiotics made once-deadly diseases (such as plague, whooping cough, diphtheria and leprosy) curable; vital in World War II". "First antibiotic" [NEW]: "The first antibiotic discovered (as NCERT states)". Likely. Q1 "state ... first antibiotic discovered" keep with the same softening.
10. Topic 03 comparison table "Effect on BOD: Little" for primary treatment. Primary settling does remove a fraction of BOD. Fix [NEW]: "Small; most of the fall in BOD is in secondary treatment". Likely.
11. Topic 03 "Why reducing BOD protects the river": "we deliver the river water that no longer triggers this oxygen crash". Wrong object. Fix [NEW]: "we deliver to the river water that no longer triggers this oxygen crash". Transcription of a book slip; certain on sense.
12. Biogas gas lists disagree inside the chapter: Topic 03 step 6 and MCQ 4 give "methane, hydrogen sulphide, carbon dioxide"; Topic 04 gives "methane + carbon dioxide + hydrogen"; Fig 8.5 gives CH4, CO2, H2S, Fig 8.6 "about 60% CH4". All real components; no value is wrong. Fix [NEW] at Topic 04 Table 4.1 row: "Methane (about 50 to 70 per cent), carbon dioxide, with small amounts of hydrogen and hydrogen sulphide" (the key points already say this). Medium.
13. Cyclosporin A: "Trichoderma polysporum" is NCERT's source; the producing fungus is now named Tolypocladium inflatum (Beauveria nivea in older papers). Copy, add a note in Table 2.4 [NEW]: "(NCERT's name; the fungus is now called <i>Tolypocladium inflatum</i>)". Possible/likely. Also Penicillium notatum (now P. rubens/chrysogenum), Propionibacterium sharmanii (now P. freudenreichii subsp. shermanii), Clostridium butylicum (butyric acid is made by C. butyricum; C. butylicum makes butanol), Anabaena azollae (now Trichormus azollae): NCERT forms, keep, note. Medium.
14. Exam statements not labelled or superlative. Topic 05 Exam Relevance "One of the single most NEET-loaded topics in the entire chapter" (the label sits only after "recur almost every year"); Topic 01 Pitfall 1 "the single most common error here"; Topic 05 Pitfall 1 "the single most common error"; Topic 03 Pitfall 1 "The most common slip"; Topic 02 Pitfall 2 "A guaranteed mark if memorised, a guaranteed loss if guessed"; Topic 04 "dependable scorer ... high marks-per-word", "Examiners love this causal chain"; Topic 05 "Examiners constantly swap these", "NEET pairs questions hinge on"; Topic 02 "asked almost every year" is labelled; "changed medicine forever". Fix [NEW]: Topic 05 "A NEET-relevant topic: ..." delete "One of the single most ... in the entire chapter"; Pitfall 1s "a very common error here (the book's view, not a counted figure)"; Pitfall 2 (T02) "A mark if memorised, a mark lost if guessed". Likely.
15. LAB wording (Topic 01). "LAB = Lactobacillus" (diagnostic list) and Table 1.1 "Lactobacillus (Lactic Acid Bacteria, LAB)": LAB is a wide group (Lactococcus, Streptococcus thermophilus, Leuconostoc too). Fix [NEW]: "<i>Lactobacillus</i> is the lactic acid bacterium (LAB) NCERT names for curd." "curd ... in minutes in a Chennai summer": curd takes hours; [NEW] "in a few hours in a Chennai summer". "Lactobacillus needs lactose" fine in context. "LAB are mesophilic" low: some curd bacteria (S. thermophilus, L. delbrueckii) are thermophilic; [NEW] "most curd bacteria multiply fastest in gentle warmth". Also Table 1.1 first-column cell "Dough leavening: bread (idli, dosa: bacteria)" sits against a row whose microbe is yeast only: [NEW] split the idli/dosa remark out ("Dough leavening: bread"; note that idli and dosa batter is fermented by bacteria). Likely.
16. Bt MCQ 4 (Topic 05) option (a) nudge "false and irrelevant to the activation mechanism" for "destroyed instantly by sunlight". Sunlight (UV) does degrade Bt spores and toxin over hours or days (the book's own Additional topic E says UV lowers efficacy), so the nudge is wrong in part. Fix [NEW]: "(a) sunlight lowers field efficacy over time but does not destroy the protoxin instantly, and it is not what switches it on." Likely.
17. Exception: Parasponia (a non-legume) is nodulated by Rhizobium. Fix [NEW]: "Rhizobium fixes nitrogen in the root nodules of legumes (the usual host, as NCERT says); with wheat it does not nodulate." Possible, low.
18. Topic 05 cry gene sentence "the gene coding this toxin is cry; it is the same gene engineered into Bt cotton". Several cry genes exist (cryIAc, cryIIAb in Bt cotton). Fix [NEW]: "The genes coding these toxins are the <i>cry</i> genes; some of them were engineered into Bt cotton." Likely, low.

20. Streptokinase (Topic 02): "dissolving the clot" and "acts as a clot buster": streptokinase activates plasminogen to plasmin, and plasmin dissolves the clot. Add [NEW]: "it activates plasmin, the enzyme that dissolves the clot". NCERT also credits genetic modification (to the Staphylococcus gene source), book omits. Low.
