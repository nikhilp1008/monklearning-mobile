# Biology 12 Ch 8 (Microbes in Human Welfare): R2 expert review of Additional topics A to G and their key points (condensed)

Scope: content/textbooks/bio-12-08/extras-a-g.ts (A..G, shared intro in A) and the Additional-topic key points (topics 6 to 12) in lib/revise/keypoints-biology-12-08.ts. Nothing edited.
Overall: transcription is faithful (5-word-shingle comparison against pp. 455-478 found only layout, page-number and formatting differences, no dropped or changed sentence); all arithmetic and every marked answer is correct. The errors are biology and wording in the book itself (overgeneral claims, one false "never covers" intro, an invented "obligate symbiont", a protoxin-in-spores slip, a "both false" approach line, superlatives).

## Flags, most serious first
1. BOD defined as "all organic matter ... over 5 days at 20 C". Where: C Practice 1 answer and F answer C1 (p.463, p.469); G Archetype 6 approach and Key Distinctions 3 (pp.473, 478). Says now: "oxygen consumed by microbes to oxidise all organic matter in 1 litre of water over 5 days at 20 C". Wrong: BOD5 measures only the part oxidised in 5 days (roughly 60-70% of the carbonaceous demand); "all" and "5 days" contradict. NCERT (and Topic 03) say "if all the organic matter were oxidised". Fix [NEW]: "BOD is the amount of oxygen that microbes would consume in oxidising the organic matter in 1 litre of water (NCERT: if all of it were oxidised). The standard laboratory test measures what is used in 5 days at 20 C (BOD5), which is only a part of this." Same sentence shortened in F C1 ("...the oxygen consumed by microbes in the standard 5-day, 20 C test") and G Key Distinctions 3. Kind biology error; source book; certain.
2. E intro false "never covers". Where: E Introduction (p.465). Says: "It does not specify the environmental conditions (temperature range, pH optimum, aerobic/anaerobic preference, humidity) under which these organisms function." Topic 05 states the alkaline gut pH that activates the Bt protoxin, that agents "depend on moisture, temperature, and soil health", free-living versus symbiotic partners; Topic 04 states methanogens need no oxygen. Fix [NEW]: "The chapter says only in words that Bt is activated by the alkaline insect gut, that Rhizobium works in root nodules and that these agents depend on moisture and temperature. It does not give numerical temperature or pH ranges for them." Kind false claim; book; certain.
3. Rhizobium "Obligate symbiont". Where: E table (p.466) and E.2 step 2 and answer (p.467); KP topic 10 does not repeat it. Says: "Obligate symbiont; requires legume host" / "Rhizobium is an obligate symbiont in root nodules". Wrong: rhizobia live free in soil (saprophytic) and fix N2 only inside nodules. Fix [NEW]: table "Symbiotic for nitrogen fixation: lives free in soil, fixes N2 only in nodules of a compatible legume"; E.2: "Rhizobium fixes nitrogen only as a symbiont in root nodules. Without a compatible legume it cannot form nodules and cannot fix nitrogen." Kind biology error; book; likely-certain.
4. Bt "spores contain a protoxin". Where: G Practice Q6 answer (p.476) (also E.1 "spores and toxin crystals" is fine). Says: "its spores contain a protoxin that is activated in the alkaline gut". Wrong: the protoxin (Cry) is in the parasporal crystal formed beside the spore. Fix [NEW]: "Bt forms a protein crystal (the inactive Cry protoxin) alongside its spores; in the alkaline gut of the larva the crystal dissolves and the protoxin is activated." Kind biology error; book; likely.
5. G Q10 answer (p.477-478): "leaves non-target insects, natural enemies and wildlife unharmed, and leaves no toxic residues". Absolute: Cry toxins are specific to groups (Lepidoptera, Coleoptera, Diptera) but kill non-target members of those groups (butterfly larvae). Fix [NEW]: "kills the target larvae effectively, is far less harmful to most non-target animals than a broad-spectrum chemical, and leaves little residue". Also in (a)(ii) "leave no toxic residues" -> "leave little chemical residue". Kind overgeneral; book; likely.
6. "No gas" in lactic acid fermentation stated universally. Where: A Method item 1 ("No gas is released"), A Standard Relationships table row 1, A ex 3 step 2 ("No CO2 is released in lactic acid fermentation"), A practice 2 and 5 and F A2 and A5; G trap "Lactic acid leavens dough" (p.473). True only of homolactic fermentation; heterofermentative Lactobacillus/Leuconostoc make CO2 and ethanol (this CO2 puffs idli and dosa batter). Fix [NEW]: "In homolactic fermentation (the equation above) no gas is released; heterolactic bacteria also make CO2 and ethanol." Short form in practice 2 answer: "No CO2 is released in homolactic fermentation: all 6 carbons of glucose end in 2 lactic acid." Table cell: "No (homolactic)". KP topic 6 point 2 already says "as written ... some bacteria also make CO2": keep. Kind overgeneral; book; certain.
7. G Archetype 3 approach line (p.471). Says: "Four answer choices: both true and R explains A / both true but R does not explain A / A true R false / both false." The item itself (correctly) prints "(d) A is false, but R is true"; the standard fourth option is "A false, R true", never "both false". Fix [NEW]: "... / A true, R false / A false, R true." Kind misleading; book; certain. (The one "both false" the brief expected.)
8. G Archetype 5 example (p.472). Says: "A = sludge (the digesting slurry) ... C = the input (dung + water)". In this chapter (Fig 8.6, Topic 04) the digesting material is "slurry" and what leaves the outlet is the "spent slurry" used as manure; "sludge" in the chapter is the sewage-plant sludge. The item makes sludge the digesting slurry and also calls the input slurry "dung + water", so A and C overlap. Fix [NEW]: "A = spent slurry (leaves by the outlet, used as manure), B = the gas collected in the gas holder (methane + carbon dioxide), C = the input (dung + water slurry)"; solution "A (spent slurry from the outlet) ...". Kind unclear/misleading; book; likely.
9. E Practice 5 and F E5 and KP topic 10 (pp.467-468). Says: "non-mycorrhizal wheat varieties"; "naturally mycorrhizal-dependent (maize, onion, most vegetables)". Wheat is normally a mycorrhizal host; non-mycorrhizal crops are mainly the Brassicaceae (cabbage, mustard), spinach and beet; "most vegetables" is untrue since cabbage and mustard are vegetables (cabbage is E.1's crop). Fix [NEW]: question "a field of a non-mycorrhizal crop such as mustard"; answer "...a non-mycorrhizal crop such as mustard or cabbage does not support colonisation ... The practice is more effective for crops that are strongly mycorrhizal, such as maize and onion." Kind biology error; book; likely.
10. C Standard Values row "Drinking water standard: Below 5 mg L-1". There is no drinking-water BOD standard; CPCB class A (drinking water source after disinfection) is BOD 2 mg L-1 or less. Fix [NEW]: "Drinking-water source (CPCB class A): 2 mg L-1 or less". Kind number; book; likely.
11. E Kalam and Method: "Each agent has an optimum temperature and pH range for activity." Contradicted by the table (baculovirus pH "Not critical"). Fix [NEW]: "Each agent works best within a temperature range, and most within a pH range (baculovirus is not pH-critical)." Also add under the table [NEW]: "These ranges are the book's typical values; NCERT gives none of them." Kind overgeneral; book; certain.
12. B table and intro. (a) Erythromycin "Moderate-spectrum" is a third class the Method (two classes) does not define; (b) the narrow-spectrum definition "either Gram-positive or Gram-negative" is a simplification (narrow can mean a few species, for example isoniazid); (c) streptomycin "Broad-spectrum": mainly aerobic Gram-negative bacilli and Mycobacterium, usually called moderate or broad by source. Fix [NEW]: erythromycin cell "Broad-spectrum (most active against Gram-positive bacteria)"; Method: "narrow-spectrum (effective against a limited group of bacteria, often Gram-positive or Gram-negative only)"; add "Spectrum labels vary between sources; NCERT does not classify antibiotics by spectrum." Kind unclear; book; possible-likely.
13. Monascus "the yeast". Where: G Archetype 1 solution (p.470). Says "from the yeast Monascus purpureus"; Key Distinctions 1 says "fungus". Monascus is a filamentous fungus (a mould). NCERT says "yeast"; keep NCERT, add the correction: [NEW] "comes from the fungus Monascus purpureus (NCERT calls it a yeast; it is a filamentous mould)". Kind biology error; NCERT/book; likely.
14. G Q8 answer (p.477): "Most industrial microbes (including Penicillium for penicillin) grow optimally at moderate temperatures (25-30 C)." Overgeneral (Lactobacillus 37-45 C, thermophilic Bacillus). Fix [NEW]: "The organisms used in fermenters (for example Penicillium, about 25 C) work at moderate temperatures; 80 C would kill them." Also option (a) "control pH using a buffer": fermenters control pH by adding acid or alkali; [NEW] "a system to control pH". Kind overgeneral; book; likely.
15. E ex 1 and practice 1 reasons for Bt at 42 C. Reason 1 (spores and crystals "less stable" at 42 C) is weak; the main losses are UV and rain wash-off. Fix [NEW]: reason 1 "high temperature on a hot afternoon dries the spray and sunlight is rich in UV, which breaks down the toxin and the spores". Kind unclear; book; possible. E ex 3 step 2: "spores germinate in response to root exudates; without these chemical signals spore germination is poor": AM spores can germinate without roots, but the hyphae stop growing and the fungus cannot complete its life cycle. Fix [NEW]: "Spores may germinate without a root, but the germ tubes cannot keep growing or form new spores without a host." Possible-likely.
16. D Method: methanogenesis equation presented as the route from organic matter. In real digesters about two thirds of methane comes from acetate (CH3COOH -> CH4 + CO2) and the CO2 + 4 H2 route is the rest; the glucose equation is an overall simplification. Add [NEW]: "In a digester, bacteria first ferment cellulose to acetate, H2 and CO2; methanogens then make methane from acetate (CH3COOH -> CH4 + CO2) and from CO2 + 4 H2. The glucose equation is the overall result." D composition "50-70% methane, 30-40% CO2" can total over 100 at the ends: [NEW] "methane about 50-70%, CO2 about 30-40% (the exact figures vary with the feed)". Calorific "~36 MJ m-3": [NEW] "about 36 MJ m-3 (lower heating value)". Kind unqualified biology; book; likely.
