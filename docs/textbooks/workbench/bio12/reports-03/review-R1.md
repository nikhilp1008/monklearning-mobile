## FLAGS

1. **"AIDS and hepatitis-B are STDs but not RTIs" (and the "not-an-RTI pair")**. Where: Topic 02, p.120 Limiting conditions; p.121 Diagnostic indicators; p.123 2.3B; Worked Q2 key (c) AIDS (p.123/125); Practice 3 (p.125); Pitfalls/cheat sheet "STD-but-not-RTI" (p.126); key point topic 1 'mistake'. Says now: "AIDS and hepatitis-B spread through sexual contact but are not infections of the sex organs, which is why 'not all STDs are RTIs' is true" while the same page defines STD "also called STI, VD, or RTI" and the cheat sheet says "STD = STI = VD = (often) RTI". Wrong: self-contradictory, and NCERT groups STI, VD and RTI as one collective set; HIV and HBV also infect/shed from the genital tract. A student who writes the book's "pair" against an NCERT-based question loses the mark. Fix [NEW]: replace the sentence in "Limiting conditions" with: "Equally, not every STD stays confined to the reproductive tract: AIDS and hepatitis-B spread through sexual contact but are systemic, blood-borne infections. NCERT uses STI, STD, VD and RTI together, so treat 'STD but not confined to the sex organs' as the book's refinement, not a separate NCERT category." Q2 stem: "...spreads through sexual contact but is a systemic (not sex-organ-confined) infection?"; keep key (c). Cheat-sheet f: "STD = STI = VD = RTI (NCERT groups them); the book singles out AIDS and hepatitis-B as systemic". Kind: biology error / internal contradiction. Source: book. Confidence: likely (certain it contradicts itself).

2. **"Only the condom protects against STIs" (overgeneral)**. Where: Topic 01 p.113 Limiting ("only the barrier method (condom) does double duty... no pill, IUD, or surgery protects against infection"); p.114 "condom's unique STI-protective bonus" (2.) ; p.119 pitfall 4 "Among all methods, only the barrier (condom) also limits STIs", cheat sheet Saheli row "Only the condom also limits STIs"; Topic 02 p.120 "the only method that simultaneously blocks conception and infection". Wrong: the female condom (femidom, in the book's own table) also reduces risk; abstinence prevents both; spermicides do not protect. Fix [NEW]: "Among the methods in this chapter, only barrier methods used as condoms (male or female) also reduce the risk of STIs; pills, IUDs and sterilisation protect against pregnancy, not infection." p.119 pitfall: "Among the methods in this chapter, only the condom (male or female) also lowers the risk of STIs." p.120: "the condom (male or female), the one contraceptive that also lowers the risk of infection." The key points already say this. Kind: biology (overgeneral). Confidence: certain.

3. **Lactational amenorrhea listed as a method that "relies on a predictable menstrual cycle"**. Where: Topic 01 p.113 Limiting conditions. Says: "Methods that rely on a predictable menstrual cycle (rhythm method, lactational amenorrhea) fail when the cycle is irregular, which is precisely when users wrongly feel 'safe.'" Wrong: LAM depends on amenorrhoea and frequent breastfeeding, not on cycle regularity; it fails when menses return or feeding drops. Fix [NEW]: "The rhythm method relies on a predictable menstrual cycle and fails when the cycle is irregular, which is precisely when users wrongly feel 'safe.' Lactational amenorrhea fails once menstruation returns or breastfeeding becomes infrequent." Kind: biology error. Confidence: certain.

4. **Hormone-releasing IUD "progesterone"; combined pill "progestogen (often with oestrogen)"**. Where: Topic 01 p.116 (3 and 4). Says: "Hormone-releasing (Progestasert, LNG-20): slow release of progesterone..."; "Combined oral pills contain progestogen (often with oestrogen)". Wrong: LNG-20 releases levonorgestrel (a progestin); a combined pill by definition has both hormones, "often" belongs to the pill group (NCERT: progestogens alone or progestogen-oestrogen combinations). Fix [NEW]: "slow release of a progestogen (progesterone from Progestasert, levonorgestrel from LNG-20) thickens cervical mucus..." and "Combined oral pills contain a progestogen with an oestrogen (progestogen-only pills, the mini-pill, contain the progestogen alone), taken daily for 21 days (combined pills), starting within the first 5 days of the cycle, then a 7-day gap, repeated." Also "Injectables... are progesterone derivatives" -> "progestogen preparations". Cheat sheet "pills 21 + 7": [NEW] "combined pills 21 + 7". Kind: biology error. Confidence: certain (LNG), likely (pill).

5. **"Beyond 24 weeks permitted only for substantial foetal abnormality"**. Where: Topic 04 p.134 Legal framework bullet, 4.3 B and D; Cheat sheet (p.137, ">24 wks only via Medical Board for foetal abnormality"); Fig 3.6 last segment. Wrong: the MTP Act (s.5) also permits termination at any gestation when a registered practitioner judges it immediately necessary to save the woman's life. Fix [NEW]: "Beyond 24 weeks: for substantial foetal abnormality, on the decision of a state Medical Board (and, at any stage, when termination is immediately necessary to save the woman's life)." Cheat sheet: ">24 wks via Medical Board for foetal abnormality (or at any stage to save the woman's life)". Figure: no change needed if the caption keeps "special categories/foetal abnormality" as the book's labels; add nothing. Kind: biology/legal, overgeneral "only". Confidence: likely.

6. **Fertile-window arithmetic does not give days 10-17**. Where: Topic 01 p.114 (3 Mechanisms 1). Says: "ovulation about day 14, the ovum survives ~1-2 days, and sperm survive ~3 days... Overlap those survival windows and the fertile period works out to roughly days 10-17". Those numbers give about days 11-16. NCERT states days 10-17 without the derivation; sperm are usually quoted up to ~5 days. Fix [NEW]: "ovulation occurs around mid-cycle (about day 14); the ovum survives a day or two and sperm can survive for several days in the female tract. Allowing for this, NCERT takes roughly days 10-17 of the cycle as the fertile period; abstaining then starves fertilisation of a partner." Kind: number/unclear. Confidence: certain on arithmetic.

7. **Viability "around the start of the third trimester"**. Where: Topic 04 p.133 (4.1 second para). Wrong: with neonatal care viability is usually put at about 22-24 weeks, the end of the second trimester (third trimester starts about week 28); also fits the 24-week legal limit. Fix [NEW]: "with modern neonatal care it is generally placed at about 24 weeks, near the end of the second trimester". (Key points already omit it.) Kind: biology. Confidence: likely.

Elsewhere "PCPNDT Act, 1994" may stay if the founder accepts the book's form. NCERT names no Act. Kind: attribution/date. Confidence: likely.
