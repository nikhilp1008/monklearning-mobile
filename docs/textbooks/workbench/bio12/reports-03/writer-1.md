## Suspected book errors / overgeneral claims (page, quote, correction, confidence)
1. p.113 and p.119/p.126 "only the barrier method (condom) does double duty ... no pill, IUD, or surgery protects against infection"; p.119 "only the barrier (condom) also limits STIs"; p.120 "the condom, which is the only method that simultaneously blocks conception and infection"; cheat sheets "Only the condom also limits STIs". Femidoms are also barrier devices that reduce STI risk (the book's own table lists "Condoms / femidoms"), and abstinence prevents both. Say "condoms (male and female)" / "the only contraceptive method that also protects". Medium-high.
2. p.116 "Hormone-releasing (Progestasert, LNG-20): slow release of progesterone": LNG-20 releases levonorgestrel (a progestin), not progesterone. Say "progesterone or a progestin". Medium.
3. p.116 "Combined oral pills contain progestogen (often with oestrogen)": a combined pill always has both; "often" fits the pill group, not the combined pill. Medium.
4. p.113 and p.114 "the ovum survives ~1-2 days, and sperm survive ~3 days ... fertile period works out to roughly days 10-17": 14-3 = 11 to 14+2 = 16 by those numbers (days 10-17 is the NCERT window; sperm may live up to about 5 days). Not wrong as "roughly", note only. Low.
5. p.113 "natural methods carry the highest failure rates", p.116 "failure rate well under 1%" (vasectomy/tubectomy): true as general statements, no source; low.
6. p.114 "Emergency contraception ... (or an IUD) used within 72 hours" and MCQ 2 key: NCERT says 72 h; copper IUD works up to about 5 days. Low.
7. p.120 "sexually transmitted infection (STI), venereal disease (VD), or reproductive tract infection (RTI)" and cheat sheet "STD = STI = VD = (often) RTI": RTI is broader (includes non-sexual, endogenous infections such as candidiasis), so not a synonym; cheat sheet hedges with "(often)". Medium.
8. p.120-126 "AIDS and hepatitis-B ... not infections of the sex organs" (the not-RTI pair): this pairing is the book's (not NCERT's); genital herpes and HPV are RTIs. Hepatitis-B is also vaccine-preventable and, with antivirals, controllable, so "not completely curable" is NCERT's wording. Low.
9. p.123 and p.125 "fertilisation naturally occurs in the ampullary region of the fallopian tube": usually the ampullary-isthmic junction (NCERT Class 12 chapter 2 says the junction). Low.
10. p.125 Practice 4: azoospermia means no sperm in the ejaculate; "if even a few sperm can be retrieved" means surgical retrieval from the testis/epididymis, which the book does not say. Not an error, a missing step. Low.
11. p.123 C "both partners are always investigated" and p.123 D "GIFT: woman who cannot produce ova": "always" is overgeneral; NCERT's GIFT wording is the donor-ovum case, in practice GIFT also uses the woman's own ova. Low.
12. p.125 Q4 / MCQ "ZIFT up to 8 blastomeres" consistent with Figures 3.3 and 3.4 captions and the p.121 table (all "up to 8 / ≤ 8"; IUT "> 8"). No mismatch.
13. Assertion-reason items in my pages: Q4 (p.118) and Q4 (p.125) and practice 5 items print the standard four options (d) "A false, R true"; no "both false" item in Topics 01 and 02.
