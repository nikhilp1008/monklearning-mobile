## FLAGS (most serious first)

1. A, Method statement, "Solving logistic growth for time", last display (book p. 633). Says now: t = (1/r) ln[ N_0(K − N_t) / (N_t(K − N_0)) ]. Wrong: the line above it gives e^(−rt) = N_0(K − N_t)/(N_t(K − N_0)), and taking ln gives −rt, so the printed t has the wrong sign (it is the negative of the true time; check N_0 = 50, N_t = 250, K = 500: printed form gives −(1/r) ln 9). The t_K/2 formula that follows is correct, and the writer's "re-derived, correct" is wrong for this line. Fix: t = (1/r) ln[ N_t(K − N_0) / (N_0(K − N_t)) ], stacked as the book (fraction inverted). Check at N_t = K/2: gives (1/r) ln((K − N_0)/N_0), as printed next. Kind: number/formula. Source: book. Certain. (The key points do not repeat the line; no other place.)

NCERT and Topic 03 (line "the fraction of carrying capacity still unused") never call it that, so the key (c) is non-NCERT and the explanation contradicts Topic 03's own wording. As N approaches K it falls to 0, which is the effect of rising environmental resistance (the limiting factors)." ## Overall judgement
The transcription of A to F is faithful and the arithmetic is right except one real formula error: the logistic time-to-N formula in A has its sign reversed (flag 1, book error), the only place a student following the text would get a wrong number. The weaker points are conceptual: the (1 − N/K) = "environmental resistance" keying (five places, a wrong MCQ answer by NCERT standards), the E demographic table and examples (Japan and Germany in Stage 4, 15–60 against 15–64, India dates), two statements in the multiple-correct example, the salinity "freshwater" gloss, five false or overstated "the chapter never covers" introductions, overgeneral Gause and functional-response claims and several unlabelled exam-frequency lines. With the [NEW] wordings above, the Additional topics are sound; the key points need only the topic 9 trap gloss and the environmental-resistance clause changed.
