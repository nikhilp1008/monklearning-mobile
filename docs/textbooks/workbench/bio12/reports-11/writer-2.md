## Suspected book errors and overgeneral claims (copied as printed)
1. p. 625 Exam Relevance (also Topic 04 text): "The most reliably tested block of the chapter", "expect 1-2 questions, almost always", "recur across years", "the single highest-return skill". Superlative and frequency claims. Labelled once at the end of the box ("the book's estimate from past papers, not official figures", our wording). Suggest: drop "most reliably" and "single highest-return".
2. p. 626 table row Competition (-, -) "Both harmed by shared limited resource" and the bullet "the fitness of the weaker is depressed". Competition can be asymmetric (-, 0); NCERT allows that only one species suffers. The book's own bullet (weaker depressed) conflicts with (-, -). Correction: add "usually" (confidence medium).
3. p. 626 / 628 clownfish and sea anemone listed as commensalism, Q1(a) "the anemone is unaffected". NCERT lists it so; most current ecology treats it as mutualism (the fish defend and fertilise the anemone). Keep NCERT, add nuance (confidence medium-high).
4. p. 626 "Commensalism ... cattle egret", MCQ 2 and pitfall 2 "genuinely unaffected": the book's own Limiting conditions (p. 625) says the 0 is approximate. Pitfall 2 "one partner is genuinely unaffected" is stronger than that. Low.
5. p. 627 "(3) ... until more than ten species vanished" (Paine's Pisaster). This is NCERT's figure; Paine's 1966 data show a fall from 15 to 8 species (7 lost). Medium confidence.
6. p. 627 "Competitive release ... proving competition was the cage". "Proving" overclaims; "showing" is safe (low).
7. p. 627 "Ectoparasites ... have comparatively simple life cycles": generalisation; some ectoparasites (ticks, some copepods and monogeneans) have multi-host or complex cycles. Add "often" (medium).
8. p. 627 "natural selection nudges successful parasites toward an uneasy truce, the 'ideal' parasite": the avirulence idea is a classic oversimplification (virulence trade-off models; virulence can evolve up). Add "often" (medium).
9. p. 627 "Yucca and its Pronuba moth": the moth genus is now Tegeticula (Pronuba is the older name). NCERT prints Pronuba; copy and note (medium-high). "the fig and its single wasp species": each fig species has its own pollinator wasp species (NCERT), not one wasp species for all figs; wording ambiguous (low).
10. p. 628 Q3 and p. 630 MCQ 3 "most likely to hold when resources are limited" fine; but Gause statement "two species competing for the same resource cannot coexist indefinitely" (p. 627), Practice 4 answer "No two species competing for the same limiting resource coexist indefinitely" (p. 630), Addendum C "Two species competing for the same limited resource cannot coexist indefinitely in the same niche" (p. 638) and Practice C3 "predicts the extinction of one species": overgeneral. Correct form: complete competitors (identical niches, one limiting resource, stable environment) cannot coexist; partial competitors can, which the book's own partitioning text says. "Extinction of one species" should be "exclusion (local extinction) of one". Only C's version carries the three conditions (p. 639).
11. p. 630 Practice 5: Assertion "Amensalism is used in medicine to produce antibiotics". The interaction is not "used"; the chemical (an antibiotic from a mould) is. Key (a) is defensible but loosely worded (medium). The item prints no options: the standard four added (see exceptions).

15. p. 636 Example B.1 (tigers): "inbreeding and chance (demographic) fluctuations" are genetic and demographic stochasticity effects, listed with the Allee effect proper (mate-finding). Fine as "extinction vortex" but not all strictly Allee (low). "no poaching" given in the stem.
