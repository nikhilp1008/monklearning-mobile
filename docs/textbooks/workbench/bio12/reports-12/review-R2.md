# Review R2, Biology 12 Chapter 12 (Ecosystem): Additional topics A to F and their key points

Files: `content/textbooks/bio-12-12/topics-4-5-extras-a-b.ts` (A, B; from line 557), `extras-c-f.ts` (C to F), `lib/revise/keypoints-biology-12-12.ts` (topic 5 to 10). ## Flags, most serious first

1. **False "the chapter never covers X" claims (book error, copied).** Checked against Topics 01 to 05 as built. All certain unless stated.
   a. Shared intro (A, intro para 1): "...the complete seral-stage sequence with specific pioneer organisms... These are precisely the layers that appear in NEET, CUET, and CBSE Board papers but are omitted from the main chapter text." Topic 04 Framework A gives both full sequences with named organisms. Fix [NEW]: "These additional topics extend Chapter 12 (Ecosystem) with five topics the chapter touches on but does not develop to examination depth: the organisms, enzymes and C:N ratio behind decomposition, the seral stages of succession in finer detail with more organisms, the steps and microbes of the nitrogen and sulfur cycles, quantitative energy-flow calculations involving ecological efficiencies, and ecosystem-service valuation with the keystone-species concept. Questions in NEET, CUET and CBSE Board papers draw on these layers."
   b. A intro para 2: "It does not identify which organisms carry out fragmentation..." False: Topic 02 names earthworms and termites as fragmenters. Fix [NEW]: "It names earthworms and termites as examples of detritivores and says that decomposers mineralise. It does not name the other organisms involved (by genus), which enzymes drive catabolism, or how the carbon-to-nitrogen (C:N) ratio of detritus governs the rate."
   c. B intro: "It does not list every seral stage in sequence with specific organism names, nor does it explain why each stage prepares conditions for the next." False on both: Topic 04 Framework A gives the sequences, names and the reason (each stage modifies its habitat). Framework D already uses "ecesis". Fix [NEW]: "The chapter defines primary and secondary succession, names the pioneer community, gives the main seral stages with example organisms, and states that both hydrarch and xerarch routes lead to a mesic climax. It does not give every seral stage and organism in full, or define nudation, ecesis and reaction as separate terms."
   d. C intro: "does not cover the nitrogen and sulfur cycles, both of which NEET examines." Topic 04 classifies nitrogen (gaseous) and sulphur (sedimentary) and has the "PuSS" mnemonic; steps and microbes are not given. The NCERT Class 12 chapter has no nitrogen or sulfur cycle (nitrogen cycle is Class 11), so "both of which NEET examines" is unsafe (likely). Fix [NEW]: "The chapter details the carbon (gaseous) and phosphorus (sedimentary) cycles and classifies nitrogen as gaseous and sulfur as sedimentary, but does not give the steps or the microbes of the nitrogen and sulfur cycles. These cycles involve specific microbial transformations that can be tested independently."
   e. D intro: "applies it through two trophic levels" is false (Topic 03 worked examples run to the 3rd, 4th and 6th levels, e.g. 48,000 to 480; 60,000 to 60 and 0.6). "the distinction between grazing and detritus food chain energy fractions" is false (Topic 02 p. 669, Topic 03 Framework C and MCQ). Fix [NEW]: "The chapter gives the 10% law (<i>E</i><sub><i>n</i>+1</sub> ≈ 0.1 <i>E</i><sub><i>n</i></sub>) and applies it along a food chain. It does not explore <b>ecological efficiencies</b> (how much of what a consumer ingests is assimilated and passed on) or <b>Lindeman’s efficiency</b> as a separate concept from the 10% rule, and it gives the grazing and detritus chains without energy fractions for each. These can appear in high-difficulty questions."
   f. E intro: "how human activities degrade ecosystem services" partly false (Topic 04 deforestation and fossil fuels raise CO2; Topic 05 "degrade one, say soil, and several others suffer"). "what a keystone species is" and "how the valuation is derived" are true. Fix [NEW]: "...It does not explain how the valuation is derived, what a keystone species is, or the mechanisms by which particular human activities degrade services."
   g. Kalams: B kalam and A kalam are labelled sentences, fine; C kalam and D kalam are from the text, fine.

2. **Costanza shares: "soil formation ~50%" and "recreation and nutrient cycling each below 10%" (E Method, E.2 question and answer, E practice 3 and answer, F Traps/drill; also Topic 05).** Book error, copied from NCERT's printed label. In Costanza et al. 1997 the largest item is nutrient cycling (about USD 17 trillion of 33, about 51%); soil formation and recreation are small. NCERT says recreation and nutrient cycling "about 10% each", the book says "below 10%" (book differs from NCERT; the two statements also cannot both hold if nutrient cycling is half). Confidence about 80% on the paper's figures, certain that the book's "below 10%" differs from NCERT's "about 10%". Fix [NEW] (keep NCERT wording, add note), E Method: "The NCERT text and its figure put soil formation at about 50% of the total, followed by recreation and nutrient cycling (about 10% each) and then climate regulation and habitat for wildlife (about 6% each). In the original Costanza paper the half-share belongs to nutrient cycling, so check the labels in your own copy; learn the NCERT figure for the exam." E.2: add the same note after the question; E.2 answer replace "costs thousands of years to replace" with "takes hundreds to thousands of years to form". E practice 3 answer: "Soil formation (~50% of total value) as NCERT’s figure labels it."

3. **"Only by weathering" in sedimentary cycles (C Method; C.2 step and answer).** Book error. Says "Nutrients return to the soil only slowly, by weathering of rock"; "released from its rock reservoir only by weathering and erosion"; answer "from rock only by weathering". Overgeneral: decomposers return phosphorus and sulfur (Topic 04 Framework C itself says so; the key point already says "mainly ... with decomposers also returning them"). Fix [NEW]: "Nutrients are released from rock slowly, mainly by weathering, and decomposers return what has entered living organisms." C.2: "...because the nutrient is released from its rock reservoir mainly by weathering and erosion..."; answer: "...released from rock mainly by weathering." Certain.

4. **C practice 6 answer (b): "nitrogen cycling is slower because the N<sub>2</sub> → NH<sub>3</sub> fixation step requires significant energy."** Book error. Contradicts Topic 04 (gaseous cycles "fast"); nitrogen is a gaseous cycle with a huge atmospheric reservoir but only a few organisms can fix N2, so the entry step is a bottleneck, not a slower cycle in the sedimentary sense. Fix [NEW]: "(b) Both are gaseous cycles with an atmospheric reservoir. Carbon cycles quickly through photosynthesis and respiration; for nitrogen, the entry of N2 into living things is limited, because only some prokaryotes (or lightning and industry) can fix it, and fixation needs a large amount of energy." Likely.

5. **D Method: "The remaining ~90% is lost as heat through respiration, excreted as waste, or egested as faeces." (D Method para 1).** Book error. Excreta and faeces stay in the ecosystem as detritus (the book's own components line: "Death (enters detritus)"). Also "energy incident at one trophic level is captured" is loose. Fix [NEW]: "Only about 10% of the energy at one trophic level is passed on to the next. Most of the rest is lost as heat in respiration; some leaves the level as faeces, excreta and dead matter, which enter the detritus pool." Likely. (D practice 1 answer "~90% is lost as heat" and kalam "incident" same wording: "...is lost, mostly as heat".)

6. **"10% is an upper limit" (D.3 title, step, answer).** Book error. NCERT: "only about 10%"; measured ecological efficiencies commonly range about 5 to 20%, so 10% is an average, not a ceiling. Fix [NEW]: title "Why 10% Is Only an Approximation"; step: "...it is an approximate average, not a fixed value or a strict upper limit." answer: "The 10% law is an approximate average; actual transfer depends on..." Likely (the key point already says "approximate average").

7. **D.2(b) and D practice 5 and answer: production (300 kJ, 80 kJ) called "available to the next level".** Book error. Per the book's own chain, production = passed on + death. Fix [NEW]: "(b) Production = 300 kJ, 30% of the ingested energy; this is the most that can pass to the next level, since part of it goes to detritus when the animal dies." Practice 5 answer: "Production = 200 − 120 = 80 kJ: the energy stored in the carnivore’s body, available to the next level." Certain on the logic.

8. **"Always upright / never inverts / physically impossible" for energy pyramids (D practice 6 q and answer; F archetype 5 rule 1; F Traps row 3; F Q5 and Q7 answers "can never invert regardless of the ecosystem"; F drill 2).** NCERT wording and exam-correct for energy per area per time, but overgeneral: a pyramid of standing stock of energy (stored biomass energy) can invert in an open sea. Do not change the exam answer; add the nuance. Fix [NEW] (once, F archetype 5 and drill 2): "The pyramid of energy, which shows energy flow per unit area per year, is always upright." D practice 6 answer: "...inverted pyramid of energy flow physically impossible..." Likely (the answer key (d) in Q7 stands).

9. **A.1 step and answer: "Trichoderma ... secretes extracellular cellulase and other hydrolytic enzymes that chemically break down cellulose and lignin into simpler inorganic compounds"; "Trichoderma secretes cellulase and ligninase".** Two errors. (i) Catabolism gives simpler organic compounds; inorganic ions come at mineralisation. (ii) Trichoderma is a cellulose-degrading fungus; lignin is broken down chiefly by white-rot fungi (for example Phanerochaete). Method para "Fungi ... ligninase" is fine as a general statement. Fix [NEW]: step: "...<i>Trichoderma</i> secretes extracellular cellulase and other hydrolytic enzymes that chemically break down cellulose into simpler organic compounds, which are later mineralised." Answer: "...earthworm fragments; <i>Trichoderma</i> secretes cellulase." Keep practice 3 ("ligninase or laccase") as is. Key point A1 already carries a "why" that corrects it; make the answer match: remove ligninase from the sentence naming Trichoderma, or add "(white-rot fungi, such as <i>Phanerochaete</i>)". Medium-high.

10. **E Method: "Ocean acidification impairs carbon sequestration by planktonic calcifiers."** Doubtful (about 60%): acidification impairs shell building; the net carbon effect is not as stated. Fix [NEW]: "Ocean acidification impairs shell formation in planktonic calcifiers and other marine organisms."
    Same Method paragraph: "Wetland drainage eliminates flood control and water purification" and practice 4 answer and practice 6 answer "eliminates": "reduces". E.1 "the entire ecosystem collapses into a bare rocky substrate": "the kelp forest is lost and the area can become an urchin barren". 11. **E practice 5: "essential to food production for approximately 75% of crop species"; "bees are small and few in number".** 75% is the share of leading crop types that benefit to some extent from animal pollination (about a third of production volume), not "essential for 75%"; bees are not few. Fix [NEW]: "Bees are small but their pollination raises the yield of about 75% of the leading crop species and is essential for some. A small, easily overlooked group with a large effect on agriculture makes them functionally critical (a keystone-level service provider)." Question: "...even though bees are small and easily overlooked". Medium-high.

## Overall judgement
The transcription of the Additional topics is faithful, with no copying slip beyond the unitalicised variable R in Topic F. The book's errors are the usual kinds: five of the six introductions claim the chapter omits things that Topics 01 to 05 actually contain; several "only", "never", "always", "eliminates" claims need scope; the 10% law is called a ceiling; production is called "available" to the next level; Costanza's soil-formation share is NCERT's mislabelled figure; and the Trichoderma and ligninase statement and the nitrogen-cycle rate answer are wrong. All the arithmetic, MCQ keys and A-R forms are correct. After the fixes above the section is safe for NEET use. The key points are accurate and already carry some corrected wording that the chapter text should now match.
