## Additional D
- Kalam: "Lindeman’s ten percent law (1942): only about 10% of the energy at one trophic level is passed on to the next." (was "incident ... captured and").
- Intro: "The chapter gives the 10% law (...) and applies it along a food chain. It does not explore <b>ecological efficiencies</b> (how much of what a consumer ingests is assimilated and passed on) or <b>Lindeman’s efficiency</b> as a separate concept from the 10% rule, and it gives the grazing and detritus chains without energy fractions for each. These can appear in high-difficulty questions."
- Method: "Only about 10% of the energy at one trophic level is passed on to the next. Most of the rest is lost as heat in respiration; some leaves the level as faeces, excreta and dead matter, which enter the detritus pool."
- D.2: question (b) "the production, with its percentage of the ingested energy"; step (b) "Production = 300 kJ = 30% of ingestion; this is the most that can pass to the next level, since part of it goes to the detritus when the animal dies."; answer "(b) production = 300 kJ (30% of ingestion), the most that can pass to the next trophic level."
- D.3: title "Why 10% Is Only an Approximation."; step "it is an approximate average, not a fixed value or a strict upper limit"; "approximately correct as an average estimate"; answer "The 10% law is an approximate average; ...".
- Practice 1 answer: "... the rest is lost, mostly as heat in respiration, with some passing to the detritus."
- Practice 5: question "Calculate its production, the energy that can pass to the next trophic level."; answer "Production = 200 − 120 = 80 kJ: the energy stored in the carnivore’s body, available to the next level."
- Practice 6 answer: "Energy flow per unit area per year decreases at each transfer ... This makes an inverted pyramid of energy flow physically impossible, ..."
