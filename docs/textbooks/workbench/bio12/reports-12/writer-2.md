## False "the chapter never covers X" claims (checked against the book's Topics 01 to 05)
- **Shared introduction (p. 692):** "the complete seral-stage sequence with specific pioneer organisms ... omitted from the main chapter text" is FALSE: Topic 04 Framework A (p. 682) gives both full sequences with named organisms (Rhizocarpon, Rinodina, Parmelia, Dermatocarpon, Polytrichum, Grimmia, Rhus; Hydrilla, Vallisneria, Potamogeton, Wolffia, Azolla, Sagittaria, Juncus, Carex), and worked example 1 (p. 683). B only splits the stages more finely and adds organisms (Lemna, Typha, Phragmites, Navicula, Chlorella, Anabaena, Zizyphus, Quercus, Betula). "These are precisely the layers ... omitted from the main chapter text" is therefore overstated. Replacement: "...with finer seral stages and further organism names".
- **B intro (p. 694):** "It does not list every seral stage in sequence with specific organism names, nor does it explain why each stage prepares conditions for the next." FALSE on both counts: Framework A (p. 682) lists the sequence with names and explains it ("Each seral stage modifies its own habitat ... better for the next community and often worse for itself"). B's "reaction" is the same idea with the term named. Replacement: "...does not name the processes (nudation, ecesis, reaction) or give every organism".
- **A intro (p. 692):** "It does not identify which organisms carry out fragmentation": FALSE: Topic 02 (pp. 667, 668) says detritivores such as earthworms (and termites) fragment, soil mites carry the breakdown further. TRUE: no enzymes named (cellulase, ligninase), no C:N ratio, no Pheretima. Replacement: "...which organisms by name".
- "The chapter introduces ... five topics" counts A to E; the sixth Additional topic F is the NEET-pattern topic: fine.
- C intro (not mine): "does not cover the nitrogen and sulfur cycles": Topic 04 names nitrogen as the second gaseous cycle and sulphur as a sedimentary example (pp. 681, 686) but does not develop them: broadly true.

## Suspected book errors and unsupported claims (page, exact words, why, correction, confidence)
1. p. 688 / p. 689 Figure 12.5 / p. 690 Ex 3 / p. 691 Q3, pitfall 2, cheat sheet: "soil formation accounts for about 50%", "the largest single contributor". This is the NCERT line, but in Costanza et al.'s own table the largest item is nutrient cycling (about 17 of 33 trillion, about half), and soil formation is small; NCERT's labels look swapped. Correction: keep the NCERT wording, add "in Costanza et al.'s paper the half-share is nutrient cycling; check the label". The same page also says "nutrient cycling < 10%", so the two cannot both hold with the original. Propagates to Figure 12.5, Ex 3, Practice 4, MCQ 3, pitfall 2, cheat sheet, mnemonic "Soil takes half". Confidence medium (about 70 per cent).
2. p. 687 / 688 "average price tag of about US $33 trillion a year ... nearly twice ... US $18 trillion": 33 over 18 = 1.83, "nearly twice" is fine. Costanza's figure was an average of a range (16 to 54 trillion); no year printed ("at the time", the 1997 paper) and none added. Low.
3. p. 681 "Missing that last point is the single most-tested error", p. 686 "The single biggest trap", p. 680 "NEET reliably draws 1–2 questions a year", p. 687 "NEET asks it only occasionally", p. 691 "the most common error": superlatives, labelled (exception 3).
4. p. 683 Ex 1, p. 685 Q1, p. 686 pitfall 2 and cheat sheet: "only they [lichens] can colonise a substratum with no soil", "never mosses or grasses". Overgeneral: cyanobacteria, other microbes and mosses can colonise rock and start soil; NCERT names lichens as the pioneers on rock. Correction: "lichens are the usual pioneers". Confidence high.
5. p. 685 Q3 option (d) "never reaches a climax" is called wrong because "both reach a climax": the book's own Limiting condition (1) p. 681 says disturbance can arrest succession at a pre-climax stage. Low (exam-level).
6. p. 692 B Practice 5 answer p. 697 "Both end in a mesic climax community, the climatic climax forest": climax need not be forest (grassland, desert in dry climates). NCERT: "progress from xeric/hydric to mesic". Correction: "toward a mesic climax community". Confidence medium.
7. p. 682 Framework D "ecesis (continuing) causes (migration, establishment ...)" versus p. 696 Example B.4 "Ecesis: the establishment and growth of the first colonisers": two senses; in Clements the continuing causes include migration, ecesis, competition and reaction; the label "ecesis causes" is loose and not NCERT. Low to medium.
8. p. 681 "Gaseous cycles ... 'perfect' ... sedimentary 'imperfect'": the perfect/imperfect terms are not NCERT (Odum). Sulphur is listed as sedimentary (NCERT does that), but sulphur has a real atmospheric phase (SO2, H2S); stated in Additional topic C by writer 3. Low.
9. p. 685 Practice 1 answer "stable net productivity": a climax has net community production near zero and maximum biomass; wording loose. Low.
10. p. 692 table: "<25:1 Fast" yet "25–30:1 Optimal (fastest, balanced)": the fastest and a faster band overlap in meaning; examples "Garden compost, manure" at 25–30:1 (finished compost is about 10 to 15:1, manure about 15 to 25:1: those are the start-mix targets). And Example A.2 calls "green legume residue" 25:1 while the table puts legume litter below 25:1. The C:N material is not in NCERT (NCERT gives lignin, chitin, N and sugars). Confidence medium; marked for the reviewer.
11. p. 693 A.1 and p. 694 Practice 3: "Trichoderma ... break down cellulose and lignin ... secretes cellulase and ligninase": Trichoderma is a cellulolytic fungus; lignin is degraded chiefly by white-rot basidiomycetes (Phanerochaete). Correction: drop "ligninase" from Trichoderma or name a white-rot fungus. Confidence medium-high. "(or laccase)" is a ligninolytic enzyme, fine.
12. p. 694 Practice 6: "Soil with a C:N ratio of 90:1": soil humus is about 10:1; the 90:1 material is the organic residue (straw). Recompute: mixing 90:1 residue with 15:1 waste to reach 30:1 needs about four times as much nitrogen from the waste as from the residue (90 + 15x over 1 + x equals 30 gives x = 4): feasible, wording loose. Confidence medium.
