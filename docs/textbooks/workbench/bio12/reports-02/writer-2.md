## Suspected book errors (page, quote, why, correction, confidence)
1. p. 82 Exam Relevance: "A consistently high-yield closing arc", "NEET loves…", "a favourite source of sequence-ordering questions". Unlabelled frequency/superlative claims; labelled once (see 8). Replace by "often asked" if the founder wants it softer. High.
2. p. 82 "Fertilisation is possible only in a short window around ovulation, and only in the female tract." False: in vitro fertilisation (this chapter's own Additional A, p. 90, IVF "outside the body in a culture dish"). Correct: "in nature fertilisation occurs only in the female tract (in vitro fertilisation is the exception)". High.
3. p. 83 "The sperm’s acrosome then releases hydrolytic enzymes that let it penetrate the zona pellucida; binding of the sperm head to the zona triggers a change in the oocyte membrane (the zona/cortical reaction)". Order: zona binding triggers the acrosome reaction, then penetration, then the cortical (zona) reaction; "zona/cortical reaction" fuses two terms. NCERT only says contact with the zona "induces changes in the membrane of the ovum". Medium.
4. p. 83 table and p. 83/89 "hCG, hPL and relaxin are produced only during pregnancy"; Relaxin source "Ovary (later pregnancy)". Matches NCERT wording (NCERT: relaxin "secreted by the ovary" in the later phase). Relaxin is also made by the corpus luteum in a non-pregnant luteal phase and by the placenta/decidua; "only during pregnancy" is a mnemonic simplification ("CHaPLeR", p. 89). Low-medium.
5. p. 83 "8–16 cells" morula, p. 84 trimester milestones (heart about one month, limbs and digits end of month two, major organ systems about 12 weeks, fine hair and eyelashes by about 24 weeks, first movements fifth month, nine months): all agree with NCERT as I recall it (high). "heard through a stethoscope" at one month is NCERT's own phrase but a heartbeat is not routinely audible by stethoscope that early (low).
6. p. 84 germ-layer table and p. 88 MCQ Q3 "mesoderm forms all the body’s muscles" (and "all muscles"): not quite; iris sphincter and dilator muscles and myoepithelial cells are ectodermal (neural crest/neuroectoderm). Correct "nearly all" or "the skeletal, cardiac and most smooth muscle". Medium. Also p. 84 "Immediately after implantation, the inner cell mass differentiates into three germ layers": gastrulation is about two weeks later (low); NCERT says the layers appear after implantation.
7. p. 88 Ex 4 "non-pregnant women do not produce it" and "only during pregnancy" (also Q1 "only during pregnancy", p. 89 cheat sheet "pregnancy-only"): hCG is also made by hydatidiform mole, choriocarcinoma and some germ-cell tumours (and trace pituitary hCG after menopause). Correct "essentially only in pregnancy (apart from rare tumours)". Medium-high.
8. p. 90 GIFT: "Eggs and sperm are collected and immediately placed into the Fallopian tube (below the blockage site)… Indicated when at least one Fallopian tube is healthy." "Below the blockage site" contradicts "healthy tube" and A.1 (blocked tubes rule GIFT out). NCERT and the book's own Chapter 3 (p. 121 table, Addendum B p. 142) define GIFT as transfer of a donor ovum (woman who cannot produce ova but can host fertilisation). Reported, not reconciled. Medium-high.
9. p. 90 IVF "Indicated when tubal blockage or severe male factor infertility prevents natural fertilisation": Chapter 3 Addendum B (p. 143, Example B.1) says for a penetration failure "IVF alone will not solve this… ICSI is the correct technique"; Chapter 2's own ICSI line (same page) is the severe male factor route. Contradiction; correction "…or unexplained/assisted fertilisation; severe male factor is ICSI". Medium.
10. p. 90 "(IUT)" is used without its expansion (Chapter 3 p. 121 gives "Intra Uterine Transfer"); AI/IUI (listed in the brief) is not in Addendum A; the intro says "the four main ART methods", Chapter 3 lists six. Not an error, a difference. 
11. p. 92 "infections of the reproductive tract" and Hepatitis B/HIV in the table: Chapter 3 p. 120 says AIDS and hepatitis B "are not infections of the sex organs… not all STDs are RTIs". Difference; correction for Chapter 2 "mainly infections of the reproductive tract (HIV and hepatitis B are exceptions)". Medium. p. 93 "always use condoms": NCERT's wording; condoms lower, not remove, risk (HPV, herpes). Low. p. 93 genital herpes "(usually type 2)" and "HSV-2": HSV-1 now causes a large share of genital herpes. Low-medium.
12. p. 91 A.3 "severe oligospermia (sperm count below 5 million/mL)": conventional cut-off for "severe"; NCERT gives no figure; Topic 01/02 sperm numbers are writer 1's. Low.
13. p. 94 B.3 "The damage is immunological, not direct pathogen lethality": chlamydia also damages epithelium directly; simplification. Low.
14. p. 93 Hepatitis B "DNA virus": true (replicates through an RNA intermediate); Hepadnaviridae true. Louise Brown 1978, Edwards and Steptoe: true (high). Candidiasis "not in the NCERT list": true.
