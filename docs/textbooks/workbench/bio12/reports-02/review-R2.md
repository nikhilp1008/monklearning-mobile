## Flags, most serious first

1. A Method, GIFT (p. 90); also Ex A.1 and A.3 (p. 91), Practice A.2 (p. 91 to 92); source: book; B; C.
 Says: "Eggs and sperm are collected and immediately placed into the Fallopian tube (below the blockage site)... Indicated when at least one Fallopian tube is healthy."
 Wrong: "below the blockage site" contradicts "healthy tube" and A.1 (blocked tubes rule GIFT out). NCERT and the book's own Chapter 3 (p. 121-122) define GIFT as transfer of a donor ovum into the tube of a woman who cannot produce ova but can host fertilisation.
 Fix [NEW]: "<b>GIFT (Gamete Intra-Fallopian Transfer):</b> Eggs and sperm are collected and placed together in a healthy Fallopian tube, so that fertilisation occurs inside the body. In NCERT's account the ovum comes from a donor, for a woman who cannot produce ova but can provide a suitable environment for fertilisation and development. A working tube is needed." Practice A.2 answer [NEW]: "GIFT suits a woman who cannot produce ova but has a healthy Fallopian tube (NCERT: ovum from a donor); prerequisite: at least one functional tube." Ex A.3 and KP A1/A2 can stay (they only use "needs a working tube").
2. A Method, IVF indication (p. 90); intro "four main ART methods" (p. 90); source: book; B/U; L.
 Says: "Indicated when tubal blockage or severe male factor infertility prevents natural fertilisation." Chapter 3 (p. 125) and this topic's own ICSI line make severe male factor an ICSI indication ("IVF alone will not solve" a penetration failure).
 Fix [NEW]: "Indicated when natural fertilisation is not possible, for example when the Fallopian tubes are blocked; for a severe male factor ICSI is used." Intro [NEW]: "...the full names, procedural steps and clinical indications of four ART methods (IVF, GIFT, ZIFT and ICSI)..." ("four main" is untrue: NCERT also has IUT and AI/IUI).
3. C Practice 2 answer (p. 97); source: book; B; L (90%).
 Says: "Progesterone-only pills suppress ovulation incompletely because oestrogen is required to suppress the LH surge reliably".
 Wrong: ovulation suppression depends on the progestogen dose; newer progestogen-only pills suppress ovulation reliably. The true reason is a low dose.
 Fix [NEW]: "Progesterone-only pills carry a low dose of progestogen, so they suppress the LH surge and ovulation less consistently than the combined pill, where oestrogen and progestogen together make the suppression more reliable; they therefore rely more on thickened cervical mucus and an unreceptive endometrium." (KP C1 already says "oestrogen makes the suppression more reliable": matches.)
4. D Method steps 3 to 6, Ex D.1, Practice D.2, KP D2 (pp. 98-99); source: book; B; L.
 Says: step 3 "hyaluronidase (breaks down hyaluronic acid between granulosa cells)", step 4 "Hyaluronidase clears the granulosa cell layer", sequence A (bind ZP3) → B → C (release of enzymes) → D → E.
 Wrong: the granulosa/cumulus layer lies outside the zona, so the sperm crosses it before it reaches and binds the zona; the enzyme released after binding cannot be what clears it first. Also cortical reaction: D step 6 says sperm entry triggers it, while Topic 03 (p. 83) says zona binding triggers "the zona/cortical reaction" (cross-topic contradiction; the KP header already refuses the fused name).
 Fix [NEW] step 3: "...acrosin (a protease), hyaluronidase (breaks down hyaluronic acid in the cell matrix between the granulosa cells; the sperm meets and crosses this layer before it reaches the zona) and other glycosidases." Step 4 [NEW]: "Penetration path: hyaluronidase has cleared the granulosa (cumulus) layer on the way to the zona; acrosin helps digest a path through the zona pellucida." Add to Ex D.1 solution: "(This is the exam sequence for the zona stage: bind, fuse, release, penetrate, enter.)" Practice D.2 answer [NEW]: "hyaluronidase: disperses the granulosa (cumulus) cells surrounding the ovum." Topic 03 p. 83 wording is the other reviewer's.
5. F Archetype 6 (p. 106), Q9 solution (p. 111), Archetype 6 example (p. 107); source: book; B; L (85%).
 Says: Mesoderm "(all muscles, bone, ...)"; "Bone and all muscles arise from mesoderm"; "(iii) Lining of the digestive system: endoderm".
 Wrong: the iris sphincter and dilator muscles and myoepithelial cells are ectodermal; the lining of the mouth and anal canal is ectodermal, so "digestive system lining" is not wholly endoderm. Keys survive.
 Fix [NEW]: "Mesoderm = movement and transport (most muscles, bone, cartilage, blood, blood vessels, kidneys, gonads)"; Q9 "Bone and most muscles arise from mesoderm"; example (iii) "Lining of the gut tube (stomach, intestine): endoderm". Same in KP F1 (already "mesoderm muscle, bone and blood": fine).
6. F Archetype 1 (p. 104), Traps row 1 (p. 107); source: book; B; C.
 Says: "Same Latin root, opposite ends of the route" and "swapping them reverses the entire sperm pathway"; "The answer is always the next station in sequence: no branching, no choice".
 Wrong: vasa efferentia (3rd station) and vas deferens (5th) have only the epididymis between them; swapping them reorders two stations, not the whole route; "always" is false (ordering items ask for the whole sequence).
 Fix [NEW]: "Same Latin root (<i>ferre</i>, to carry), but the vasa efferentia leave the rete testis and the vas deferens leaves the epididymis." Trap cell: "Seen in NEET: swapping them breaks the order of the sperm pathway." Approach: "...The answer is the next station in the sequence: the route does not branch." (The KP header already avoids these.)

8. E table (p. 100), Ex E.1 (p. 101); source: book; B/N; L.
 Says: Ovulatory (day 14) row: Oestrogen "Peak (triggers LH surge)"; FSH "Surge" day 14, GnRH "Surge", luteal GnRH "Suppressed". Text: oestrogen switch "about days 12-13"; E.1: oestrogen and FSH peak day 13, LH day 14.
 Wrong: a peak on day 14 cannot trigger a surge that is already under way; days disagree inside the topic (12-13, 13, 14). GnRH is released in pulses (frequency rises mid-cycle): "surge" is not standard (NCERT gives none), "suppressed" overstates the luteal slowing. E.1 FSH "low days 1-5, rises steadily to day 12" simplifies (NCERT: gradual rise through the follicular phase, peak with LH mid-cycle), acceptable but day 13 vs 14 should match.
 Fix [NEW]: ovulatory row Oestrogen "Peak just before (days 12-13); triggers the LH surge"; GnRH cells "Pulses more frequent (drives the surge)" and luteal "Pulses slowed"; FSH "Peaks with LH (day 13-14)"; E.1 "FSH ... peaks on day 13 to 14, with the LH peak on day 14". Mark the table "a simplified pattern".

## Overall judgement
The transcription of A to F is accurate, the keys are right, and the key points are sound (they already correct several book slips). The book's own errors cluster in three places: the GIFT and IVF definitions in A (contradicting NCERT, Chapter 3 and itself), the oestrogen-required mini-pill reason, and the acrosome-reaction order and day labels in D and E; the rest is overgeneral wording ("all muscles", "opposite ends", "never", "Seen in NEET") that should be softened. Most are one-line fixes; none changes a marked key.
