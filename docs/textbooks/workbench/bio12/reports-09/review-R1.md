## FLAGS

1. Topic 04, Key procedures, 4.3.5 (book p. 507). Says now: "selectable markers (antibiotic-resistance genes) are used to kill off non-transformants so only cells carrying the recombinant DNA survive and are recovered."
   Wrong: antibiotic selection keeps every cell that took up the vector, recombinant or not (Topic 02 2.3.2 itself says a plasmid without insert grows on both plates). Only a screen such as insertional inactivation separates recombinants.
   Fix [NEW]: "…are used to kill off non-transformants, so only cells that carry the vector (with or without the inserted gene) survive; a further test such as insertional inactivation then picks out the cells that carry the recombinant DNA. The selected cells are recovered and first grown on a small scale…"
   Kind: biology error. Source: book. Confidence: certain.

2. Topic 02, Key procedures, "How a restriction enzyme recognises and cuts" (book p. 489). Says now: "Read the top strand left-to-right (GAATTC) and the bottom strand left-to-right (also GAATTC reading 5′→3′)."
   Wrong: the bottom strand is printed 3′–CTTAAG–5′; read left to right it is CTTAAG. GAATTC is read right to left. A student told "left-to-right" learns that the bottom strand spells GAATTC.
   Fix [NEW]: "Read the top strand left to right, 5′→3′ (GAATTC); read the bottom strand right to left, which is its 5′→3′ direction, and it is also GAATTC."
   Kind: biology error / unclear. Source: book. Confidence: certain.

3. Topic 01, Worked question 3 (NEET), Pitfall 4, Cheat sheet row 4, KP topic 0 point 4 (book pp. 484, 485, 486). Says now: option (b) "a native plasmid of <i>Salmonella typhimurium</i>" is the answer, "which acted as the self-replicating vector"; pitfall "The first rDNA used a plasmid of Salmonella typhimurium, not E. coli."
   Wrong: the book (p. 481 steps 1 and 2) and NCERT say the resistance gene was cut out of a plasmid of Salmonella typhimurium and then joined to a native plasmid (the vector); the recombinant was put into E. coli. So Salmonella is the source of the gene, not the vector; the question asks what the gene was linked "with" (the vector) and its marked answer contradicts the chapter's own step 2. The KP file already words this correctly ("resistance gene cut from a Salmonella plasmid").
   Fix [NEW] (stem and solution): "Stanley Cohen and Herbert Boyer constructed the first recombinant DNA by cutting an antibiotic-resistance gene out of a plasmid of which bacterium, and linking it to a native plasmid vector?" with the options kept as bacteria; or keep the options and rewrite the solution: "The resistance gene came from a plasmid of Salmonella typhimurium and was ligated to a native plasmid that served as the vector; the recombinant plasmid multiplied in E. coli." Pitfall [NEW]: "Naming the wrong organism. The resistance gene came from a plasmid of Salmonella typhimurium; the recombinant plasmid was introduced into E. coli, the host that cloned it."
   Kind: wrong MCQ answer / biology error. Source: book. Confidence: likely (NCERT wording recalled, not re-read; the chapter's own step 2 is the certain part).

4. "Same enzyme only" rule, overgeneral. Places: Topic 02 Concept intuition "Limiting Conditions" ("only works cleanly when both… cut by the same enzyme"); Worked Q4 A-R; Topic 04 Concept intuition Limiting Conditions, 4.3.4 ("will only join a vector also cut by EcoRI"), table 4.2.1 "(same enzyme for both)", Practice 5 ("must be cut with the same enzyme"), cheat sheet "Same enzyme rule", Topic 02 mnemonic. (book pp. 487, 493, 503, 507, 510, 511)
   Wrong: enzymes that leave the same overhang (BamHI/BglII/Sau3AI, XbaI/SpeI) join, and blunt ends join any blunt end. The rule is NCERT's working rule, so keep it but qualify it once per place.
   Fix [NEW]: add "(or by enzymes that leave compatible ends)" at 4.3.4: "a fragment generated by, say, EcoRI will only join a vector with matching overhangs (one also cut by EcoRI, or by an enzyme that leaves the same overhang), because only then do the overhangs match"; Practice 5 answer end: "…the vector and insert must be cut with the same enzyme (or enzymes that leave compatible ends)." EcoRI vs BamHI (AATT vs GATC) is correctly incompatible: the answer stands.
   Kind: biology (overgeneral). Source: book. Confidence: certain that it is overgeneral.

5. "First restriction enzyme = Hind II" (Topic 02 table 2.2.1, MCQ 1 stem "first restriction endonuclease to be discovered", pitfall 1, cheat sheet; Topic 01 none) (pp. 488, 494, 495).

NCERT says Hind II; keep for the exam, but say so. Also "Hind II" (not "Hind III") is a different enzyme from HindIII listed in pBR322 in the same topic: say it once.
   Fix [NEW]: table cell "first one discovered (NCERT): Hind II"; MCQ 1 solution add "(the first type II enzyme characterised; Hind II is a different enzyme from HindIII)". Name form: HindII or Hind II as the page prints, consistently.
   Kind: unclear / attribution. Source: book (NCERT idiom). Confidence: likely.
