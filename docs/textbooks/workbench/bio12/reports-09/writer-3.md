## Suspected book errors and weak points (page, exact words, why, correction, confidence)
1. p. 524 "Restriction enzymes, PCR and gel electrophoresis are the three most heavily tested sub-topics": superlative from unverifiable data; also the Distribution table lists them with "Very high/Very high/High", cloning vectors also "High". Copied, labelled. Medium.
2. p. 525 "The answers are fixed and do not change year to year": overgeneral (facts such as figures and enzymes are updated or re-framed). Low.
3. p. 525 "zero-reasoning marks" and p. 528 "the highest-difficulty questions": loose superlatives. Low.
4. p. 526 archetype 3 example prints option (d) "Both A and R are false" (fourth option not "A false, R true"); changed per the instruction, reported. Also the same page says "(depending on the paper...)". High.
5. p. 533 "The highest value is always denaturation": true for standard PCR; "always" is overgeneral only in principle. Low.
6. p. 520 and p. 523 "Each individual’s pattern is unique (except identical twins)"; Practice 6 "VNTR patterns are individually unique except for identical twins": a multi-locus VNTR profile is unique in practical terms only (very small match probability), not absolutely. Practice 6 answer opens "Suspect 2 is the source" though it then says "consistent with identity": should read "Suspect 2 cannot be excluded; the profile is consistent with him being the source". Also "a DNA profile from the same person must match in every band" ignores partial or degraded samples. Medium.
7. p. 520 "(14–100 bp)": VNTR (minisatellite) repeats are usually given as about 10 to 100 bp (some texts 9 to 80); 14 is a lower figure used in one source. Low-medium.
8. p. 522 Example C.4 "US companies patented neem-derived pesticides": the contested neem patent was granted by the European Patent Office to W. R. Grace and the US Department of Agriculture (1994, revoked 2005); the contested turmeric patent was a US patent held by two university researchers (revoked 1997), basmati by RiceTec (US). Suggest "a US company and the US government patented ... (European Patent Office)". Medium.
9. p. 522 Example C.3 "AIDS can be diagnosed by both ELISA ... and PCR": ELISA and PCR diagnose HIV infection; AIDS is a clinical diagnosis (NCERT itself says "AIDS" for the ELISA test). Low.
10. p. 521 table "Detects femtogram quantities or less" and "2–3 hours": order-of-magnitude figures not in NCERT. Low.
11. p. 522 Example C.2: the question lists five steps but the book's correct sequence has six (adds electrophoresis, which is not an option); the final answer "4 → 2 → 3 → 1 → 5" is consistent with the five listed steps (recomputed). Copied; note that question and answer differ in length. 
12. p. 529 archetype 6 (b): "The bacterial transcription/translation machinery reads the intron-containing DNA directly": more exactly the introns are transcribed into pre-mRNA that bacteria cannot splice; and "most likely" hides other reasons (codons, promoters). Low.
13. p. 530 Q1 "Hind II (Hamilton Smith, 1970) was the first type II restriction endonuclease characterized": supported (Smith and Wilcox, 1970; Nobel 1978 with Arber and Nathans). Confidence the attribution is right: high. The hint "before the EcoRI naming convention was even established": the naming convention (Smith and Nathans, 1973) did follow it: consistent. Type I enzymes were characterised earlier (Arber, Meselson, 1968), so "first type II" is correct and "first restriction endonuclease" in the question stem is loose.
14. p. 531 Q4 "R = strain RY13": correct (NCERT: E. coli RY13). "I = first enzyme from that strain": correct.
