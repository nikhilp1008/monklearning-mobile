## FLAGS (most serious first)

1. Same-enzyme ligation rule is overgeneral (propagates). Where: D Traps table row "Same-enzyme rule for ligation" (p. 529, correct-answer cell); D Graded Q9 answer (p. 532, last sentence); KP topic 7 "mistake" ("fragments joined without the same enzyme"); (also Topic 04 4.3.4, Topic 04 cheat sheet and practice 5, KP topic 1 "Cut Same" line: other share).
   Says now: "Vector and insert must be cut with the same restriction enzyme for sticky ends to match"; "The vector and insert must be cut with the same restriction enzyme to generate matching sticky ends."
   Wrong: the real rule is complementary ends. Different enzymes with compatible ends (BamHI/BglII/Sau3AI, all GATC) ligate; blunt ends ligate too. NCERT's teaching rule is "same enzyme", so keep it, but do not state it as the only way.
   Fix: [NEW] Trap cell: "Vector and insert must be cut with the same restriction enzyme (or enzymes that leave compatible sticky ends) so the ends match". [NEW] Q9 last sentence: "The vector and insert are normally cut with the same restriction enzyme (or with enzymes that leave compatible ends) so that the sticky ends match." [NEW] KP D mistake: "fragments joined although their ends do not match (the usual rule is the same enzyme for both)".
   Kind biology error (overgeneral); source book; certain.

3. "Any plant species" / "cereals are not Agrobacterium hosts" (B).
   Where: B Method, Biolistics ("Widely used for cereals and monocots that are not susceptible to Agrobacterium infection"); B Example B.4 steps and ans ("many cereals (maize, wheat, rice) are not natural hosts ... lack the wound-response signals that activate the vir genes ... suitable for any plant species regardless of susceptibility"); B Practice 3 answer ("works for any plant species, including non-host cereals"); KP already avoids this.
   Wrong: Agrobacterium transforms rice, maize and wheat routinely (acetosyringone, immature embryos); monocots were merely hard (recalcitrant) historically. "Lack the wound-response signals" is not a correct mechanism; biolistics is not "any species" either (regeneration limits). Biolistics is also used for animal cells and chloroplasts.
   Fix [NEW] B.4 steps: "Biolistics (gene gun or particle bombardment) is used. Cereals such as maize, wheat and rice were historically hard to transform with Agrobacterium (monocots respond weakly to wounding and release few of the phenolic signals that switch on the vir genes), so DNA-coated particles fired through the cell wall gave a way that does not depend on the host's response to the bacterium." [NEW] B.4 ans: "Biolistics/gene gun; cereals were hard to infect with Agrobacterium, and biolistics does not depend on the host's response to it." [NEW] Practice 3 answer: "(i) It does not depend on the host's response to Agrobacterium, so it can reach cereals that respond poorly; (ii) it can deliver DNA to intact tissues or cells without wound induction." Biolistics sentence: "Widely used for cereals and other monocots that were hard to transform with Agrobacterium."
   Kind biology error (overgeneral); source book; certain.

4. C Practice 6 answer states a certainty it cannot.
   Says: "Suspect 2 is the source. All 10 bands matching the crime scene is consistent with identity ... a DNA profile from the same person must match in every band."
   Wrong: a full match of 10 bands is consistent with identity, not proof; and an exact match is expected only for intact samples. Opening verdict contradicts the next clause.
   Fix [NEW]: "Suspect 2 cannot be excluded: all 10 bands match the crime-scene profile, which is consistent with suspect 2 being the source (a profile is unique in practice, except for identical twins, but a match is strong evidence, not absolute proof). Suspect 1 is excluded as the source: 3 of the 10 bands do not match, and a profile from the same person matches at every band. The 70 % band sharing is not proof of identity; it suggests only that suspect 1 may be biologically related to the person who left the sample." Also C Method step 6 "Each individual's pattern is unique (except identical twins)" -> [NEW] "Each individual's pattern is, for practical purposes, unique (except identical twins)". KP C2 "A sample matches a person when every band matches" -> [NEW] "A sample is consistent with a person when every band matches (strong evidence, not absolute proof)".
   Kind biology error/overstatement; source book (KP also); likely.

5. HIV is an RNA virus: "PCR detects DNA directly" (C).
   Where: C table (Target "DNA or RNA" but Principle "specific DNA sequence"); C Example C.3 ("PCR measures viral load (number of HIV RNA copies...)"); C Practice 2 answer "(i) PCR detects DNA directly"; C.3 stem "AIDS can be diagnosed by both ELISA ... and PCR".
   Wrong: HIV RNA is reverse-transcribed first (RT-PCR; quantitative RT-PCR gives viral load); PCR can also detect proviral DNA. ELISA and PCR diagnose HIV infection; AIDS is a clinical diagnosis (NCERT itself loosely says "AIDS" for the ELISA test, so keep for CBSE answers but fix the mechanism).
   Fix [NEW] Practice 2 (i): "PCR detects the viral genetic material itself (HIV RNA after reverse transcription, or proviral DNA), so it works during the window period before antibodies appear". [NEW] table PCR Principle: "Enzymatic amplification of a specific DNA sequence (RNA is first copied to DNA by reverse transcriptase)". [NEW] C.3 stem: "HIV infection (leading to AIDS) can be diagnosed by both ELISA ... and PCR". Kind biology error/imprecision; source book; likely.

6. Retroviral vector overstatements (A).

   Wrong: integrated provirus is copied to daughter cells, but expression can be silenced and random integration can disrupt host genes; "all ... can express" is overgeneral.    Fix [NEW] (b): "Yes, the gene is expected to be passed to and expressed in the daughter cells ... so every daughter cell carries it, and it is normally expressed (though a provirus can sometimes be silenced)". Kind biology error (overgeneral); source book; likely.

9. Selection vs screening: terminology conflicts with NCERT and with the chapter's own wording (B, D, KP).
   Where: B Method ("antibiotic resistance is selection; lacZ disruption is screening"); D Values item 2 calls pBR322 insertional inactivation "two screening systems"; Topic 02 heading "Selecting recombinants: insertional inactivation"; Topic 04 4.3.5 "selectable markers ... kill off non-transformants so only cells carrying the recombinant DNA survive"; KP B1 "Antibiotic resistance is selection; lacZ disruption is screening".
   Wrong: NCERT calls blue-white (chromogenic substrate) and insertional inactivation both "selectable markers" for recombinants. Strictly, plating on ampicillin selects for vector-bearing cells; picking the amp-resistant, tet-sensitive colony needs replica plating (a screening step). The distinction is legitimate but the chapter uses "selecting recombinants" elsewhere, so a CBSE student is told two things.
   Fix [NEW] one line after the B Method distinction: "NCERT calls insertional inactivation and the chromogenic (blue-white) test selectable markers for recombinants; in this strict sense they are screening steps that follow the antibiotic selection. Use NCERT's wording in a CBSE answer." Kind unclear/misleading; source book; likely.

## Overall judgement
The transcription of A-D is faithful and the declared deviations are all allowed; the arithmetic and every marked MCQ key are correct (one key, D Q5, has a defensible alternative). The weak points are the book's own: overgeneral rules (same enzyme, any plant species, cereals not hosts, "only for bacteria", unique profiles, "all daughter cells ... express"), a few wrong or loose facts (HIV/PCR as DNA, neem patent, VNTR length, electroporation hosts), terminology that conflicts with NCERT (selection vs screening), two false "chapter never covers" introductions, and inconsistent gene-symbol italics. The key points are cleaner than the chapter text and mostly need small edits.
