## Suspected book errors (page, text, correction, confidence)
1. p. 190 Q2 option (c) "two alleles of one gene", answer says "(c) is multiple allelism": two alleles is ordinary allelism; multiple allelism needs more than two. Reword "(c) describes ordinary (two-allele) allelism". High.
2. p. 185 "first explored by Galton (1883)": doubtful; Galton studied continuous variation in the 1880s but the multiple-factor explanation is Nilsson-Ehle (1909) and, for skin colour, Davenport (1910s). Medium.
3. p. 189/190 Practice 4 and p. 188 Ex 4: definition p. 186 says polygenic = "three or more genes", yet Example 4 and Practice 5 call a two-gene trait "polygenic". Say "two or more" or "a two-gene model". High.
4. p. 193 "(2) Only mutations in the germ line ... are passed to offspring" and p. 198 "Only germline mutations pass to the next generation", cheat sheet: overgeneral; somatic mutations pass to daughter cells and via vegetative propagation. Add "in sexually reproducing organisms". Medium-high.
5. p. 195 "mutation is the only one that creates genuinely new alleles" and p. 192/198 "original/ source of all new variation": horizontal gene transfer and intragenic recombination also introduce new alleles/combinations. Medium.
6. p. 193/198 "Polyploidy ... caused by failure of cytokinesis": allopolyploidy (defined in the same bullet) arises from hybridisation then genome doubling. Add "mainly". Medium.
7. p. 186/190 "continuous traits never give clean Mendelian ratios": overgeneral ("seldom"). Medium. p. 186 "cannot be improved or predicted by tracking a single gene": breeding does improve them by selection; reword "cannot be explained by". Low-medium.
8. p. 205 C.2 "exposed to fetal Rh antigen only during delivery": sensitisation can also occur in late pregnancy, miscarriage, trauma (NCERT says "during delivery"). Medium. p. 205 "governed by a single gene": the Rh system is two linked genes (RHD, RHCE); fine as NCERT simplification. Low.
9. p. 202 Test D: "If no such case appears, the trait is likely recessive": absence of the case is weak evidence; rely on Test R. Low. p. 204 Practice 2 "every generation, both sexes equally => autosomal dominant" is the likely, not the only, reading. Low.
10. p. 196 Example 3 / p. 197 practice 5: 90 and 180 count codons of the original frame downstream of the deletion; ignores that the new frame may hit an early stop. Fine for the level. Low.
11. p. 199 Overview names four derived ratios; 13:3 and 9:6:1 are not covered (not an error).
12. p. 188 top: "1/2." is the orphaned tail of Procedure B step 4 (page split), joined back.
13. Name currency: book prints "fetus/fetal" (NCERT "foetus"), "sensitised"; copied. Overview prints "Hardy-Weinberg" with hyphen as the brief said.
