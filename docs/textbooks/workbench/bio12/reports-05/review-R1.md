## Flags, most serious first

1. **PCR placed before restriction digestion; not part of the NCERT procedure.** Where: Topic 06, Key processes, proc "The DNA fingerprinting procedure" step 2 (p. 268); Pro-Tip "I-PRESHA"; cheat sheet "Steps"; Fig 5.8 box 2; key points topic 5 (steps, shortcut). Says now: "Amplification by the Polymerase Chain Reaction (PCR) to make many copies: optional; greatly increases sensitivity, so even a tiny sample suffices." Wrong/misleading: NCERT's steps are isolation, restriction digestion, electrophoresis, blotting, hybridisation, autoradiography; PCR is a modern addition, and if the VNTR locus is PCR-amplified with flanking primers the digestion and Southern steps are usually not needed. Amplifying whole DNA and then digesting it is not a standard sequence. Fix [NEW]: step 2 "Amplification (optional, a modern addition to NCERT's list of steps): PCR makes many copies of the DNA or of chosen VNTR regions, so even a tiny sample suffices; PCR-based typing often replaces the digestion and blotting steps." Cheat sheet [NEW]: "Isolation → (PCR, optional modern addition) → Restriction digestion → ...". Kind: biology/unclear. Source: book. Confidence: likely.

2. **Human gene count ~30,000 and related counts (dating).** Where: T06 p. 265 text, "Salient features", Q1 model answer, practice 1, MCQ 3 (key "30,000"), pitfall 1, pro-tip, cheat sheet, kalam; key point T6 mentions 20,000 already. Says: "about 30,000 genes, far fewer than the 80,000–140,000 once guessed". Current estimate about 20,000 protein-coding genes (newer NCERT editions give a lower figure than 30,000). Chromosome 1 (2968), Y (231), 1.4 million SNPs are NCERT-edition figures; current databases give different, larger SNP counts. Fix [NEW], once, after the "Salient features" bullet: "(NCERT's figure; the current estimate of protein-coding genes is about 20,000, so a question may print either; answer with the number the question's own text gives.)" Keep the MCQ key. Kind: number/dating. Source: book (and NCERT). Confidence: certain on the discrepancy.

3. **Hershey–Chase: "did not" become radioactive is too absolute.** Where: T02 Key processes 3.3 "bacteria infected by 35S-phages did not (the protein coat stayed outside)"; Fig 5.3 Track 2 "No radioactivity in the cells (protein stays outside)", pellet "(not radioactive)"; MCQ 3 nudge (a). 5. **Translation overstatements (writer 2 items, ruled below).** T04 Key processes, "Why the start and stop signals matter": (a) "it can never “drift” between frames once initiation has set it" (programmed ribosomal frameshifting exists); (b) "a functional mRNA must contain AUG to be translated at all" (bacteria also start at GUG, UUG); (c) "The stop codons, having no tRNA, are the only built-in “end” signal" and Worked Q4 "the stop codon never is [coding]" (UGA can encode selenocysteine in special contexts). Fixes [NEW]: (a) "it does not normally drift between frames once initiation has set it"; (b) "which is why an mRNA normally needs an AUG to be translated (bacteria occasionally start at GUG or UUG, which the initiator tRNA still reads as methionine)"; (c) "The stop codons, having no tRNA, are the usual built-in “end” signal" and "(the trap is counting three amino acids by treating every triplet as coding: a stop codon codes for none here)". Kind: biology. Source: book. Confidence: likely.

6. **Initiation described with the eukaryotic mechanism in a bacterial-ribosome topic.** T04 Initiation: "The mRNA binds the small ribosomal subunit, which scans to the start codon AUG." Fix [NEW]: "The mRNA binds the small ribosomal subunit, which comes to sit on the start codon AUG." (key point T3 "scans to AUG" the same). Kind: biology/unclear. Source: book. Confidence: likely.
