## The book, its layout, and how Class 12 differs from Class 11 (read this part twice)
- **Chapter openers.** Each chapter's title page carries a LaTeX contents box that runs on to the next page (not copied). The chapter's first topic often **starts low on the same page as the contents spill** (Chapters 2 to 6, 10, 11 and 13: the heading and its Exam Relevance box sit mid-page); every later topic, and every Additional topic header, starts at the top of a page unless the briefs say otherwise. Chapter 1 has "Part I" to "Part IV" instead of numbered sections; Chapter 12 prints its five topics as "Chapter 1" to "Chapter 5"; Chapter 8 has an unnumbered front note.
- **Eight sections per topic**, with the names the chapter brief lists (they differ by chapter, and by topic inside a chapter). Chapter 7 Topic 01 has only six. The headings are printed with numbers ("1.2 Key Structures, Definitions & Roles", or "Section 2: …"): the number is dropped, the name is the part. The topic's opening "Exam Relevance:" box is the first `p` of "Concept intuition".
- **MCQ and worked-example options are LETTERS (a) to (d)** in this book (the Class 11 book used numerals): keep the book's letters. Bold in running text (`<b>(a)</b>`), plain `label` text in `opts`. The book's "Correct: (c)" or "Answer: (b)." means option c, b: `correct` is the letter's position (a = 0, b = 1, c = 2, d = 3). Check every one. Practice items carry level tags ("[CBSE]", "[CUET]", "[NEET]", "[NEET high-difficulty]") and are numbered 1. 2. …; their answers are printed together afterwards in one paragraph ("Answers: 1. … — 2. …" or "Answer Key: 1. … — 2. …"): each answer goes on its own `practice` item as `ans`. Match-the-column answers read "A–3, B–1, C–2". Worked examples are "Example 1 — [CBSE Board · 3 marks] …" with "Model answer:", "Reasoning:" or "Fast reasoning:" lines and an "Answer: (b)." line. Level tags in brackets: `tag` is the text inside the brackets.
- **Assertion-Reason** items print their key as "(a) both true, R is the correct explanation of A; (b) both true, R not the correct explanation; (c) A true, R false; (d) A false, R true." ## Biology notation (the house form for every Biology chapter; the founder may amend it, see DECISIONS-B12 9 to 11)
- **Scientific names are italic**, as the book prints them: genus capitalised, species lower case, the whole binomial in one `<i>`: `<i>Homo sapiens</i>`, `<i>Pisum sativum</i>`, `<i>Plasmodium vivax</i>`; the abbreviated genus after first use as the book writes it, `<i>E. coli</i>`. A genus alone is italic (<i>Rhizobium</i>). Names above the genus (families, orders, classes, phyla: Poaceae, Mammalia, Chordata) are upright, capitalised. An author's name after a binomial is upright. A common name is plain text. Never italicise a word for emphasis, a term being defined, or a book title. Where the book italicises an English word for emphasis, keep the word and drop the italics (BRIEF-12-07-08).
- **GENETICS: gene and allele symbols are italic, everything else upright.** A gene or allele symbol (T, t, R, r, Rr, RrYy, S<sub>1</sub>, the lac genes i, z, y, a) is `<i>…</i>` whole: `<i>Tt</i>`, `<i>RrYy</i>`, `<i>I</i><sup><i>A</i></sup><i>i</i>` (the letter italic, its superscript allele name italic), `<i>X</i><sup><i>h</i></sup><i>Y</i>`, `<i>lacZ</i>` as the page prints it. **Upright:** phenotypes and phenotype classes ("A", "AB", "O" blood groups, "tall", "red"), the words homozygous and heterozygous, generation labels with real subscripts (P, F<sub>1</sub>, F<sub>2</sub>), chromosome names (X, Y, Z, W as sex chromosomes, "chromosome 21", "XX", "XY", "XO"), karyotype numbers (45, XO), protein and enzyme names (β-galactosidase), promoter P and operator O when the page prints them upright, restriction-enzyme names as printed, DNA and RNA base letters. If the page prints something in italic that this rule would set upright, or the reverse, copy the page and list it. **Ploidy: 2<i>n</i>, 3<i>n</i>, <i>n</i>** (digit upright, <i>n</i> italic), `2<sup><i>n</i></sup>` for gamete numbers.
- **Crosses and genotypes in running text** are written on one line with the book's symbols: `<i>Tt</i> × <i>Tt</i> → 1 <i>TT</i> ∶ 2 <i>Tt</i> ∶ 1 <i>tt</i>`; gametes as `½ <i>T</i> + ½ <i>t</i>` with stacked fractions. **Punnett squares are `table` blocks**: the first row holds the column gametes, the first column the row gametes (headed by the cross), each cell the genotype; label the table with the book's caption or the cross. A pedigree or linkage map the book prints as text is written as a labelled table or list (generation, individual, sex, status), never as bare symbols.
- **Ratios** use the book's "∶" (U+2236, "3 ∶ 1", "9 ∶ 3 ∶ 3 ∶ 1", "1 ∶ 2 ∶ 1 ∶ 2 ∶ 4 ∶ 2 ∶ 1 ∶ 2 ∶ 1") with a space on each side; "1:1" typed with a colon is not used. A probability or frequency is a stacked fraction or a decimal as the book prints it.
- **Equation variables** (population N, r, K, B, D, I, E, t; Hardy–Weinberg p and q; species–area S, C, A, z; energy E, N<sub>t</sub>, N<sub>0</sub>) are italic with real subscripts and superscripts, as in Mathematics: `N<sub><i>t</i></sub>`, `<i>p</i><sup>2</sup> + 2<i>pq</i> + <i>q</i><sup>2</sup> = 1`, `d<i>N</i>/d<i>t</i>` written as a stacked fraction. Units are upright.
- **DNA, RNA and restriction notation:** strand ends with the prime U+2032 (5′, 3′), never an apostrophe; a sequence as upright capital letters on one line in the book's spacing (5′–GAATTC–3′), the cut position marked as the book marks it; fragment sizes in bp, kb, kDa; "EcoRI", "HindIII", "BamHI" as the page prints them (plain, unless the page italicises the genus letters). A figure label with such a sequence keeps the same form.
- **Chemical formulas and ions are upright**, with real subscripts and superscripts: CO<sub>2</sub>, O<sub>2</sub>, H<sub>2</sub>O, CH<sub>4</sub>, NH<sub>3</sub>, Ca<sup>2+</sup>, ³²P as `<sup>32</sup>P`. Reaction arrows → ⇌ as the book prints them.
- **Units and sizes** with a real micro sign: μm (U+03BC), nm, mm, cm, m, kg, g, mg, mL, mol, °C, K, %, bp, kb, kDa, ppt, mya. A value is kept with its unit by a no-break space. Compound units are written with real superscripts, **never a slash**: g m<sup>−2</sup> yr<sup>−1</sup>, kcal m<sup>−2</sup> yr<sup>−1</sup>, ind. yr<sup>−1</sup>. Multiplication ×, ranges with an en dash (2–3), minus −, ≈, ±.
- **Symbols**: ♂ ♀ if the book prints them (check the fonts test: if a glyph is missing, report it), ×, →, ⇒, ⇌, ∶, ′, °, Greek letters as real Greek (α, β, μ). Never the PDF's mathematical alphanumeric letters (U+1D400–U+1D7FF).
- **Bullets and flow chains the book writes with arrows in text** ("stigma → style → ovary") are text with → (U+2192). A real drawn flow chart or tree is a figure.
- **Quotation marks** typographic as printed, the right way round.
- **FRACTIONS and NO EM DASHES** (the founder's house rules for every new subject, DECISIONS-B12 1): every fraction is `<frac><num>…</num><den>…</den></frac>`, never `a/b`, never `½` typed as one character; the text carries **no em dash (—)**: the book uses them everywhere as a plain separator (about 300 per chapter), so write a colon, commas, a bracket or a full stop (a range keeps its en dash). **The checks fail on both.** Every em dash you replace is a change to the book's text: say once in your report that you did it and report any where the sense could change. A title with an em dash ("Key Processes & Frameworks — Causes of Biodiversity Loss") is plain text: write a colon in place of the dash and list it. A part heading like "Derivations — Exponential and Logistic Growth" is handled the same way.
- Plain-text fields (topic `title`, `kalam`, part titles, example `tag`, table and defgrid titles) take no markup and no Unicode subscript or superscript letters (so a title with CO₂ is written "CO2" in the plain field only if the page prints it that way; check what the page prints).

## Lessons from the Class 11 reviews (every Biology 12 chapter: do these now so reviewers need not)
- **Read the page, not only the text dump.** The Class 11 writers dropped a "⇒ disqualified" the dump showed; check every sentence you copy, every table cell and every label against the page image (the PDF with the Read tool). Genetics and equation pages scramble most in the dump: read them on the image.
- **Overgeneral claims in the book** ("only", "all", "every", "always", "never", "mutually exclusive", "the whole system was introduced by …", "the only type that…", unsupported superlatives "the most-tested …", "the single densest block", "every NEET cycle", "a guaranteed scorer"): copy as printed and report each with page, why, and the exact correction you would make. These were the biggest class of error in Biology 11, and this book has about 10 to 25 "never" and 4 to 22 "always" per chapter. Rules in biology have exceptions (autogamy and geitonogamy, "pyramids are always upright", "linkage is never complete", "the father determines sex", "RNA enzymes do not exist"): say what the exception is.
- **Attributions, dates and "NCERT says …"**: if the book gives a person, a year, a law or an NCERT sentence you cannot confirm, report it. Do not trust "canonical NCERT position". Dating and attribution care (the Woese lesson): three-domain claims, "first", "discovered by", geological and hominin dates (mya), the years of laws and conventions (MTP 1971 and 2021, Earth Summit), the IUCN "2004 baseline", the human gene count (about 20,000 now, about 30,000 in older NCERT): where the book and NCERT or current work differ, copy the book and report both values with your confidence; never add a year the book does not print.
- **A claim that "the chapter never covers X" or "mentions only X"**, in an Additional topic introduction (every Addendum here begins "The chapter introduces … but does not …"): check it against the topics as built (with the topic numbering of the brief) and report each false one with the true replacement. The Class 11 chapters had many.

## Slashes in Biology (checks, 2026-11-16)
The one-line fraction check for Biology flags only a number over a number (or over a bracket, π or a root), e.g. "3/4", "0/1", "5/6", "9/16".
