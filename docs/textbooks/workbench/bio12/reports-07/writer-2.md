## Suspected book errors and unsupported claims (page, exact words, why, correction, confidence)
1. p. 373 "morphine is extracted from the latex of the poppy, not the leaf or fruit" (4.2.1 text; also Cheat Sheet "latex"). Opium latex is tapped from the unripe seed capsule, which is botanically the fruit. Correction: "from the latex of the poppy (tapped from the unripe capsule), not from the leaf". Confidence medium-high. NCERT only says "latex of poppy plant".
2. p. 372 "barbiturates, amphetamines and benzodiazepines are genuine sedatives and tranquillisers" (also repeated p. 378 only for barbiturates/benzodiazepines). Amphetamines are stimulants, not sedatives; NCERT lumps the three as drugs "used as medicines to help patients cope with mental illness like depression and insomnia". Keep the NCERT wording, add: amphetamines are stimulants. Confidence high.
3. p. 372 "Addiction ≠ dependence: one is psychological, the other physiological" and the table (Addiction "Psychological", Dependence "Physiological"). This is the NCERT-level split; in modern medical use dependence has a psychological part too. Keep, add nuance. Confidence medium.
4. p. 376 Q4 answer "both are opioids from Papaver somniferum": heroin is semi-synthetic (made from morphine from the poppy), as the same answer says earlier; loose. Confidence medium.
5. p. 373 "(≈ 5% in beer, 10% in wine, 40% in whisky)": not NCERT; typical beer 4 to 6%, wine 9 to 15%, whisky about 40%. Acceptable approximation. Low.
6. p. 372 "the most vulnerable window" for adolescence, p. 378 "the most common MCQ loss", p. 373 "the single highest-yield grid", p. 378 "Almost every objective question": superlatives (labelled, see 3 above).
7. p. 380 "Type I always mentions IgE, mast cells, or histamine. Type III always mentions 'immune complex'": "always" is a question-wording heuristic, not a biology rule; rule (1) "antibodies against a cell surface … it is Type II" also catches antibody-mediated receptor diseases the book later lists (Graves', myasthenia gravis in B.3), which the classic scheme places under Type II/V. Correction: "usually". Confidence medium.
8. p. 382 "Streptococcus pyogenes (causes rheumatic fever)": the bacterium causes strep throat; rheumatic fever is the autoimmune sequel (B.2 says "following a throat infection"). Correction: "(a throat infection that can be followed by rheumatic fever)". Confidence high.
9. p. 383 "Rheumatoid arthritis is systemic" with RA in Type III (practice B.3): RA damage is also T-cell and cytokine driven (Type IV); "Type III hypersensitivity contributes" is acceptable. Low.
10. p. 385 "Malnutrition … the single largest cause of secondary immunodeficiency worldwide": widely accepted but an unsupported superlative. Low. p. 385 SCID "the most frequently named primary immunodeficiency in NEET": labelled.
11. p. 385 Example C.1 answer prints only "As above." and A.4 "See table above." (no real answer line); kept, reported.
12. p. 388 "nearly all cases are HPV-related" (cervical cancer): true clinically (about 99%), not an overgeneral slip. p. 388 D.3 "T2 (tumours 2–5 cm)": the cut-offs are above 2 cm up to 5 cm; boundary loosely stated. T2 N0 M0 = Stage IIA is correct (recomputed). Low.
