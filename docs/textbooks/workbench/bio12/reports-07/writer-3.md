## Suspected errors in the book (page, quote, why, correction, confidence)
Antibiotics and STIs
1. p. 392 (F Method): "Four major mechanisms of action." The table has four rows but only three mechanisms (cell wall; protein synthesis, two rows 30S and 50S; nucleic acid). Say "three mechanisms, four targets" or add a fourth (folate or membrane). High.
2. p. 392: "Each class attacks a specific bacterial structure or process absent in human cells … toxic to bacteria but relatively safe for humans." Overgeneral: ribosomes, DNA gyrase and RNA polymerase exist in human cells (and mitochondria) in different forms; the targets are different, not absent; aminoglycosides, chloramphenicol, tetracyclines have human toxicity. Correction: "structure or process that differs in human cells". High.
3. p. 393 (F.2): "Because tetracycline does not bind the 80S ribosome, it selectively inhibits…without affecting human cells." Tetracyclines bind 80S weakly and inhibit mitochondrial (70S-like) synthesis; "unaffected" (answer, p. 394) overstated. Medium-high.
4. p. 393 Key exam rule and p. 394 F.3: cell-wall drugs "are bactericidal"; "Bacteriostatic antibiotics … target the ribosome". Not a rule: streptomycin (aminoglycoside, 30S, listed on p. 393) is bactericidal; tetracycline and erythromycin can be bactericidal at high concentration or against some species. Add "in general". Medium-high.
5. p. 393 "Penicillium notatum": current name *P. rubens* (Fleming’s strain); "the original antibiotic" is better "the first antibiotic discovered". Rifampicin is semi-synthetic from rifamycin (source now *Amycolatopsis*). Medium.
6. p. 394 (F.4): "the recipient bacterium is permanently resistant"; "resistance can emerge … within days". Overgeneral/unsupported: plasmids can be lost, and emergence times vary. High (permanently), medium (days). Also "receives a copy through the sex pilus" simplifies (DNA transfers through the mating channel the pilus sets up). Low.
7. p. 393: four "ways bacteria acquire resistance" overlap (enzymes and efflux genes are usually acquired by HGT). Low.
8. p. 391 (E.2): "the most common infectious cause of female infertility"; "up to 70% of infected women". Tubal damage from chlamydia is "a leading preventable infectious cause"; the 70% is a common figure (about 70% women, about 50% men), unlabelled. Medium-low.
9. p. 390: "its most dangerous feature" (chlamydia asymptomatic) is a superlative opinion. Low-medium.
10. p. 392 (E practice 3): "co-infection rates are 15–40%": literature range is roughly 10 to 40%. Low. Practice 5: "visible only by dark-field microscopy" is overgeneral (silver stains and immunofluorescence also show it). Medium. E.3 lists Gram stain/culture for gonorrhoea; NAAT is the preferred test for both. Low.
First aid (safety: report, copy as printed)
11. p. 395 DRABC and p. 396 to 397 CPR: "begin CPR if no pulse"; "When a victim has no pulse and no breathing". Current Indian, AHA and ERC guidance: lay rescuers start CPR on an unresponsive person who is not breathing normally, without a pulse check (unreliable), and call for emergency help first. "Call for help" is missing from DRABC. High.
12. p. 396 Heimlich: abdominal thrusts only. Standard first-aid courses give 5 back blows first for a conscious adult, and an infant or a pregnant or obese victim needs a different technique (chest thrusts); no "call for help". High.
13. p. 396 Burns: second-degree "do not break blisters; cover loosely" omits cooling (also 20 minutes under running water). p. 398 "heals in 2–3 weeks" holds for superficial partial-thickness only. Medium-high.
14. p. 396 and 397 Dog bite: "do not apply anything to the wound" and "Cover with a clean dressing". WHO and NCDC advise washing for 15 minutes then applying an antiseptic (povidone-iodine or alcohol) and not bandaging tightly. Vaccine schedule "days 0, 3, 7, 14, and 28" is the IM Essen schedule (valid); shorter WHO-approved schedules exist. Medium-high.
15. p. 395 RICE "ice … 15–20 minutes every hour": current guidance is 15–20 minutes every 2 to 3 hours; newer protocols (PEACE and LOVE) discourage prolonged rest and ice. Medium. Nosebleed "Sit upright": usual advice is sit leaning forward, pinch 10 to 15 minutes. Medium.
16. p. 395 "Five high-frequency exam scenarios" and the intro "tested by CBSE Boards and CUET": exam-frequency claims, labelled, unverified.
What NEET Actually Asks
17. p. 399 (Archetype 1): "eliminates three wrong options in under 10 seconds" (unsupported speed claim); "A bacterial disease cannot match a protozoan entry" is fine. The Typical question prints no (a) to (d) options; the key is a pairing. p. 398 Distribution: "a dependable source of marks", "4–6 distinct MCQ types", and p. 402 "two highest-frequency targets": unsupported, labelled.
18. p. 399 Archetype 2: "the only Ig that does" and Q5 "the only class that crosses the placenta": true for humans in NCERT-level biology (IgG, via FcRn); mention "in humans". Low. "IgA crosses in milk" means is secreted into milk.
19. p. 400 table: Effect column is not parallel (Cannabinoids "Cardiovascular", Belladonna "Anticholinergic", others a class); NCERT calls *Atropa belladonna* and *Datura* hallucinogens. "LSD … Claviceps purpurea" is semi-synthetic from ergot alkaloids (the book says so). Low.
20. p. 402 transmission "five buckets": not exclusive or exhaustive (ringworm also by towels and fomites; HIV and hepatitis B also sexual and vertical; ascariasis and typhoid are faecal-oral; sexual contact has no bucket). Medium.
21. p. 403 Archetype 8 worked answer: "Option (b) matches verbatim." The WHO wording is "…and not merely the absence of disease or infirmity"; (b) is a paraphrase. Low.
22. p. 403 Traps: row 1 "any option naming a male vector in a vector-borne disease is wrong": true for mosquitoes (only females bite) but male tsetse flies and triatomine bugs also bite; restrict to mosquito-borne diseases. Medium-high. Row 5 "Chemically it is acetylmorphine": heroin is *diacetylmorphine* (as p. 401 says); "acetylmorphine" alone is monoacetyl. High. Row 8 "always RNA → DNA for HIV": reverse transcriptase also makes the second DNA strand from DNA. Medium. Row 9 "sporozoites are in salivary glands": sporozoites form in the oocyst on the gut wall and then move to the glands; the gut does hold sporozoites briefly. Low-medium. Row 4 "only passive is fast" is fine within acquired immunity (innate is fast).
23. p. 404 Q1: tetanus "toxin detection" is not routine (clinical diagnosis). Low.
24. p. 405 Q4: "RBCs contain the asexual blood stages (merozoites, gametocytes)". Gametocytes are the sexual stage (precursors of gametes); correct "asexual stages (trophozoites, schizonts, merozoites) and gametocytes". High.
25. p. 405 Q6: benzopyrene "initiates cancer by mutating proto-oncogenes … converts proto-oncogenes into permanently active oncogenes": it forms DNA adducts that mutate several genes, notably the tumour-suppressor *TP53* and *KRAS*; the single-pathway statement is overgeneral. Medium. The stem "key chemical carcinogen" is also a superlative.
26. p. 406 Q8: the A-R pair is fine but the explanation (innate immunity alone) leaves out adaptive immunity; low. Q9: "AIDS patients do not die from HIV itself" overgeneral (HIV-associated cancers, wasting, dementia, nephropathy). Medium. Q10 fine (CO affinity about 200 times that of oxygen).
27. p. 407 Q12 is the same question as Archetype 6 (p. 401), labelled "Hard" though one-concept recall. Q11: B-cells "not the primary mechanism" is fine (antibody-mediated rejection exists). The graded set is labelled "increasing order of difficulty", but Q1 to Q7 are Easy or Medium and Q8 to Q12 all "Hard".
28. p. 398 G Q: "5–6 cm deep, 100–120 per minute" matches AHA adult guidance (5 to 6 cm); fine.
29. p. 399 Archetype 3: "cripples the entire adaptive immune system": overstated ("much of"). Low.
