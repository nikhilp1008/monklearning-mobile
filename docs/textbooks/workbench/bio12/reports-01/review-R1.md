## Flags (most serious first)

**1. Double fertilisation "found only in flowering plants. No other group on Earth does this"**
- Where: Topic 03, Concept intuition (p. 23); also "Unique to angiosperms" in Key structures (p. 23), Cheat sheet row 1 "unique to angiosperms" (p. 28), Worked example 1 model answer and "Full-marks tip" (p. 27).
- Says now: "This is double fertilization, and it is found only in flowering plants. No other group on Earth does this."
- Wrong: *Ephedra* and *Gnetum* (Gnetales, gymnosperms) show a second fusion in the egg apparatus (second sperm with the ventral canal nucleus), so "no other group on Earth" is false. What is unique is that the second fusion makes endosperm (triple fusion). NCERT itself says "unique to flowering plants", so keep the exam phrase but qualify it once.
- Fix [NEW]: "This is double fertilization, the hallmark of flowering plants (NCERT calls it unique to them): the second fusion makes the endosperm. A second fusion also occurs in <i>Ephedra</i> and <i>Gnetum</i>, but it does not make endosperm." In the other places keep "unique to angiosperms" (it is the NCERT answer); no change needed there beyond this one note.
- Kind: biology error (overgeneral). Source: in the book. Confidence: certain (facts); the fix wording is a judgment call.

**2. "Parthenogenesis = an animal term" (and "animal egg → parthenogenesis")**
- Where: Topic 03 "Don't confuse" box (p. 27) and Common pitfalls bullet 4 (p. 28); Topic 04 "Limiting conditions" (p. 30), Common pitfalls bullet 1 (p. 35), Cheat sheet row "Distinguish" (p. 36).
- Says now: "parthenogenesis = an animal term (egg → organism), not the angiosperm seed/fruit story"; "animal egg → parthenogenesis"; "parthenogenesis = animal term."
- Wrong: parthenogenesis (development of an egg without fertilisation) occurs in plants and algae. The book itself describes it in Topic 04: diplospory ("whose diploid egg grows into a diploid embryo", p. 31, step 3 of 3.1 "The diploid egg develops into a diploid embryo without fertilization") and "non-recurrent apomixis ... a normal haploid egg ... develops into an embryo without fertilization" (p. 32). A student who learns "animal term" will be wrong on those lines. The three-way contrast (seed / seedless fruit / egg) is still the right exam distinction.
- Fix [NEW] (use in all five places, shortened where space is tight): "parthenogenesis = an egg developing into an embryo or organism without fertilisation (the usual example is animals, such as honeybee drones; in plants the same step occurs inside some apomixis routes). It is not the seed-or-fruit term." Cheat row: "parthenogenesis = egg develops without fertilisation (animals; also a step in some apomixis)."
- Kind: biology error / misleading. Source: book (Topic 04 reuses Topic 03). Confidence: certain. (The key points already soften this; the chapter text does not.)

**3. Apomixis "without meiosis and without fertilization": absolute, and "no need for pollinators"**
- Where: Topic 04: Concept intuition (p. 30), Key definitions bullet 1 (p. 30), 3.1 "Apomixis removes both" and steps 1 to 4 (p. 31), 3.5 "no need for pollinators" (p. 32), Ex 1 model answer, Ex 4 and Pitfalls bullet 2 ("it skips both"), MCQ Q1 solution, Cheat sheet row 1 (pp. 34 to 35).
- Says now: "Apomixis ... produces seeds without meiosis and without fertilization (syngamy)"; "true-breeding seed, no need for pollinators".
- Wrong/incomplete: (i) NCERT defines apomixis only as "formation of seeds without fertilisation"; "without meiosis" is true of diplospory (mitotic) but adventive embryony does not involve the megaspore mother cell at all, and non-recurrent apomixis (the book's own 3.2) follows meiosis. (ii) Most aposporous and many diplosporous apomicts are pseudogamous: pollination is still needed to trigger the egg or to form endosperm, so "no need for pollinators" is false for them. (iii) Step 1 of 3.1 ("a cell that would normally undergo meiosis instead divides mitotically") describes diplospory only, not apospory or adventive embryony.
- Fix [NEW]: Key definitions: "Apomixis: a special mechanism in flowering plants that produces seeds without fertilisation of the egg (NCERT definition). In the usual routes the embryo sac or embryo forms without meiosis, so the seedlings are clones of the mother." 3.5: replace "no need for pollinators" with "no fertilisation of the egg needed (though many apomicts still need pollen to form the endosperm)". 3.1 step 1: "In diplospory a cell that would normally undergo meiosis instead divides mitotically, keeping the full diploid (2<i>n</i>) set." Keep "skips meiosis and syngamy" as the exam answer for the A-R item but add once: "(true of the usual routes)".
- Kind: biology error (overgeneral). Source: book. Confidence: likely (facts certain, pseudogamy certain; severity moderate because the NEET key follows the book).

- Kind: biology error / misleading. Source: book. Confidence: certain. The key point (topic 0 shortcut) already says "a cell made by fusion, such as the zygote, has its own value": good.

## Overall judgement
Faithful transcription of a book that is mostly NCERT-correct in its core biology: the ploidy ladders, the 7-celled/8-nucleate embryo sac, the 3n endosperm calculation, the hybridisation order, the true/false fruit and testa/tegmen/perisperm items, and all 16 MCQ keys are right. The problems are the book's overstatements and one wrong teaching line: "parthenogenesis is an animal term" (contradicted by the book's own Topic 04), "double fertilisation is found only in flowering plants / no other group", the absolute "without meiosis and without fertilisation" for every apomictic route, the "ploidy rule" that makes every post-meiosis cell n, the "no enzyme degrades" exine claim, the "oldest seed" and "tallest flower" superlatives, and exam-frequency superlatives that were only partly labelled. The transcription itself adds only two upright *Yucca* names and a few consistency slips. The key points have already been written around most of these (flags 1, 3, 5, 6, 7, 8, 9, 18, 19 are handled there), so the chapter text and the key points currently disagree on those points; correcting the chapter text as above removes the disagreement.
