5. Chemoattractants (C Method "Chemotropism", KP topic 6 first point; p. 44, book).
Says: "Key chemoattractants include calcium ions (Ca2+), boron, and small peptides secreted by the synergids."
Wrong: the attractants identified are the cysteine-rich LURE peptides from the synergids (plus small molecules such as GABA and nitric oxide in the pistil). Ca2+ is the tip-growth ion inside the tube (a Ca2+ gradient at the tip), boron promotes germination and tube growth; neither is a "chemoattractant" from the ovule in the usual teaching. Chemotropism is not in NCERT Ch 1.
Fix [NEW]: "The best-established attractants are small peptides (LURE peptides) secreted by the synergids. Calcium ions and boron are needed for pollen germination and tube growth." KP: "The attractants named are small peptides secreted by the synergids; Ca2+ and boron support tube growth". Likely.

6. Second function of the filiform apparatus "aids discharge of male gametes" (C Method, Ex C.3, Practice 3 "aids the mechanical discharge", F, KP topic 6 second point; p. 44, 45, 46, 53).
Wrong/unsupported: NCERT and standard texts give one function, guiding the pollen tube into the synergid; the other function usually listed is that its folded wall (transfer-cell like) increases surface for absorbing nutrients into the synergid. Release of the sperm cells follows synergid degeneration/rupture, not the apparatus. "Mechanical discharge" is not standard.
Fix [NEW] (C.3 answer): "(i) Guiding the pollen tube into the synergid (chemotropism); (ii) its wall folds increase the surface for absorbing and passing nutrients into the synergid." Remove "aids discharge" in Method, Practice 3, F and KP topic 6. Possible-to-likely; at least tell the founder the second function is not NCERT.
