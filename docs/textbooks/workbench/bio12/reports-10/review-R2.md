## Flags, most serious first

**1. F Level 3 Q9, statement (iv) and key (p. 595, key p. 598): the keyed answer rests on an unsupported claim.**
- Says now: "(iv) α-1 antitrypsin produced in transgenic sheep is used to treat emphysema." Key (b) "(i), (iii) and (iv)"; answer line "(i), (iii) and (iv) are correct."
- Wrong: NCERT names the protein (α-1-antitrypsin, used for emphysema) and says nothing of sheep. Sheep-milk α-1 antitrypsin was an experimental product, never an approved treatment; the medicine in use is plasma-derived. A student who knows this can mark (iv) false and then no option is right.
- Fix [NEW]: statement "(iv) The human protein α-1 antitrypsin, used to treat emphysema, has been made in transgenic animals." Keep the key (b) and the answer line unchanged.
- Kind: wrong MCQ answer risk / biology. Source: book. Confidence: likely.

## Overall judgement
The Additional topics are a faithful copy of the book: no sentence was lost or changed beyond the allowed liberties, every MCQ key and every recomputed number is right, and the introductions make no false "never covers" claim. The weak points are the book's own: a questionable keyed statement (transgenic sheep in Q9), the GEAC name, invented exam shares and unlabelled frequency language in F, a protocol that fits only the indirect ELISA, an annealing range that disagrees with Chapter 9, over-flat claims about insulin routes, retroviral vectors and the Nagoya Protocol, and a neem date that is the final one rather than the first. The key points for these topics are accurate and cautious, with two exceptions (the order of the shares and the confusing GEAC sentence). Fixing items 1 to 6 would remove nearly all risk to a NEET student.
