# Review 2 (Opus, observations/reagents/procedures vs the NCERT lab manual): Chemistry 12 Ch 12, 2026-11-19

Verdict: no wrong colour, precipitate, gas, reagent or indicator colour anywhere. Flags are one source conflict decided the wrong way, one inconsistency between topics, and wording. Syllabus coverage (cations, anions, titrations, four experiments, six preparations) matches NEET UG 2026 Unit 20 exactly.

1. **Layer test "not disturbed by cyanide and sulphide" (Topic 01, part "Lassaigne's test: observations and calculation", paragraph "Why the halogen test needs boiling with HNO3", last sentence):** that half is Tandon's; NCERT boils the extract with conc. HNO3 first if N or S is present, and the chapter's own procedure says so. Fix: "The sulphide and cyanide must be removed first, for the silver nitrate test and for the layer test alike." Certain.
2. **Silver halide colours differ between Topic 01 and Topic 04, and Topic 01's note ("older books") is wrong** (NCERT itself uses both wordings): Topic 01 table "Tests on the sodium extract" bromine and iodine rows and note, example 1 step (d), the exam trap "Colours of silver halides are close"; Topic 04 confirmatory-test table and the exam trap "Silver halides in ammonia". Replace the note with: "The NCERT manual uses two wordings for the same precipitates. In its Lassaigne section it calls AgBr yellow, partially soluble, and AgI dark yellow, insoluble. In salt analysis (Topic 04) it calls AgBr pale yellow, soluble with difficulty, and AgI yellow, insoluble. Learn pale yellow AgBr and yellow AgI; the layer test settles a doubtful case." Optionally the Topic 01 exam trap: "AgBr is pale yellow and dissolves only partly (with difficulty), AgI is yellow (dark yellow in the Lassaigne wording) and does not dissolve". Certain.
3. **Weak nitrogen test (Topic 01 formula "Prussian blue (nitrogen test)", note):** "the blue stays colloidal and the solution only looks green" is not NCERT's. Fix: "With only a little cyanide, a green solution forms at first and deposits Prussian blue on standing." Likely.
4. **Carboxylic acids and the FeCl3 test (Topic 01, "Hydroxyl groups: observations and chemistry", "Result, precautions and errors", last sentence):** unsupported. Fix: "A compound that turns blue litmus red may be a phenol or a carboxylic acid; the NaHCO3 test below separates them." Likely.
5. **Concentrated H2SO4 called a "stronger" acid (Topic 04, "Anions: tests with concentrated sulphuric acid", first paragraph):** fix: "Concentrated sulphuric acid is non-volatile and an oxidising agent: it drives out the more volatile HCl and HNO3 from chlorides and nitrates, and oxidises bromide and iodide further to the halogen." Likely.
6. **Calorimetry sign (Topic 05 first paragraph "ΔH = q_p = mcΔT"):** same as review 1 flag 4: q = mcΔT is the heat taken up by the solution; ΔH = −q. Certain.
7. **NCERT's calorimeter formulas multiply W by 4.184 again (Topic 05, the "second route, as the manual does" steps):** the text is right; add one line to the formula note: "The manual's printed expression multiplies W by 4.184 as well; since W is already in J K−1, it is added after multiplying the mass by 4.184, as here." Certain.
8. **NaOH and HCl results ignore NCERT's four-decimal rule that the text cites (Topic 03 "Result" paragraphs):** "0.0995 M; strength 3.9801 g L−1" (0.09950 x 40) and "3.6869 g L−1" (0.10101 x 36.5). Certain.
9. **Significant figures vs stated uncertainty (Topic 05 CuSO4 example, "How firm is it?" and the answer):** 20 % uncertainty supports two figures: "…so the value is good to about two figures, about +13 kJ mol−1." Keep 12 570 J mol−1 as the arithmetic. Likely.
10. **Stopwatch source of error (Topic 05 kinetics, "Sources of error"):** "changes every time by the same amount" is wrong: "Starting the stopwatch at a different point of the mixing in different runs makes the times inconsistent; start it when half of the solution from B has been added, every time." Likely.
11. **Iodoform exam trap reasons backwards (Topic 02, "Iodoform: exam traps", last item):** "Iodoform needs several moles of iodine per mole of compound (three for acetone, four for ethanol), so the reagent is added until the iodine colour persists, which shows it is in excess." Certain.
12. **Mislabelled exam-trap heading (Topic 02, "p-Nitroacetanilide: exam traps", last item):** "Amine first, nitro second." -> "Acetylate first, nitrate second." Certain.
13. **Thiocyanate written two ways (information):** NCERT uses both; optionally add to one note "both forms are written for the blood-red thiocyanate complex."
