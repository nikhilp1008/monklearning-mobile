### Topic fields

- `n`: `'01'`, `'02'`, … for the book's sub-topics; `'A'`, `'B'`, … for the Additional Topics.
- `title`: the book's own sub-topic or addendum title, exactly (for an addendum, the text after
  "Addendum A — "). Commas are fine.
- `extra: true` on every Additional Topic, absent on the others.
- `kalam`: one sentence that says what the topic is about, shown in the "up next" row. Take it
  from the book, verbatim: the first sentence of the topic's opening text if it states the idea
  in under ~140 characters, otherwise the book sentence that does. No `chip`, no `hook`.

### Parts: the book's own sections

Every topic's `blocks` start with a `{ t: 'part', title }` and are cut into parts exactly at the
book's section headings. Everything in a section goes in its part, in the book's order.

- Sub-topics have "Section 1: Concept Intuition" … "Section 8: Cheat Sheet Box". The part title
  is the book's section name without "Section N:", in sentence case, `&` written as "and", and
  without the word "Box": "Concept intuition", "Key formulas and definitions", "Key procedures
  and logical framework" (Section 3 is named differently in some sub-topics: use what that
  sub-topic says, e.g. "CBSE board derivations"), "Worked examples", "Practice exercises",
  "Crack the MCQ", "Common student pitfalls and pro-tips", "Cheat sheet".
- Additional topics have run-in headings ("Method", examples, "Practice — Addendum A"). Use the
  book's own headings as part titles ("Method", "Worked examples", "Practice"); the opening text
  before the first heading is a part titled "Introduction".

### Which block for which book element

- Prose paragraph → `{ t: 'p', html }`. Several consecutive paragraphs may share one `p`,
  separated by `<br><br>`. A displayed (centred) equation in the middle of prose goes on its own
  line inside the `p`, with `<br>` before and after it.
- A bold run-in heading inside prose ("**Worked derivation (pendulum period).** Let us…") stays
  as `<b>…</b>` at the start of the paragraph.
- A term the book defines in its "Key Formulas & Definitions" section in definition form →
  `{ t: 'def', term, html }`. If it is just a paragraph, keep it a `p`.
- A two-column table → `{ t: 'defgrid', title, rows: [{ k, v }] }`. A table with three or more
  columns → `{ t: 'table', title, head: [...], rows: [[...], ...] }` (first column is the row's
  name). Use the table's own title if it has one; otherwise `title: null` for `table`, and for
  `defgrid` the book's lead-in words.
- A key formula → `{ t: 'formula', kicker, main, legend, note }`. These also build the chapter's
  Formula sheet (`lib/revise/formula-cards.ts` — read it, especially `formulaName`, `items`,
  `meaning`, `isWordRule`, `keepTogether`). `kicker` is the formula's name as the book gives it;
  `main` is the formula line(s) joined with `<br>` ("label: formula" is fine); `legend` is the
  book's "where …" / symbol lines, one string per symbol, written `symbol = meaning`; `note` is
  the validity condition or remark the book attaches, or null. After writing, run the formula
  card builder over your topics (see "Checking your work") and make sure every card reads right.
- Numbered steps of a method → `{ t: 'proc', title, steps }`. A step with a bold lead is
  `"<b>Lead.</b> rest"`.
- A step-by-step derivation the book sets as equation + reason → `{ t: 'deriv', kicker, steps:
  [{ eq, why }] }`. If the book writes it as prose with displayed equations, keep it as `p`.
- A worked example → `{ t: 'ex', tag, q, steps, ans }`. `tag` is the book's level without
  brackets: "CBSE Board Level", "NEET Speed Trap", "JEE Main Level", "JEE Advanced Level",
  "CBSE / NEET Level". `q` is the "Problem." text. `steps` are the solution's lines/paragraphs in
  order, one string each. `ans` is the final answer line. A "Check." paragraph is the last step,
  as `"<b>Check.</b> …"`. "Pitfall to remember: …" after an example is a further final step,
  `"<b>Pitfall to remember:</b> …"`. Keep examples consecutive: the reader turns a run of `ex`
  blocks into one swipeable set.
- Practice exercises + "Final Answers" → `{ t: 'practice', items: [{ q, a }] }`. `q` keeps the
  book's level tag at the start, e.g. `"[JEE Main] The density of a metal is …"`. Pair each
  question with its answer by number.
- "Crack the MCQ" → one `{ t: 'mcq', q, opts, correct, solution }` per question, consecutive.
  `opts` are the book's options in order, `label` without "(A)". `correct` is the 0-based index
  of the book's answer. The book's "Answer:" paragraph explains the right answer and then each
  wrong option: put the right answer's explanation in `solution` (starting with the book's
  words after "Answer: (C) coulomb."), and the sentence(s) about each wrong option in that
  option's `nudge`, word for word. The correct option's `nudge` is `null`. If the book discusses
  two wrong options together ("(A) and (D) are …"), give both options that same text.
- "Common Student Pitfalls" numbered list → `{ t: 'mistakes', items }`, one item per pitfall, the
  bold lead as `<b>…</b>`. Each "Pro-Tip" → `{ t: 'protip', html }`, keeping its label, e.g.
  `"<b>Pro-Tip (speed + accuracy):</b> For any conversion …"`.
- "Cheat Sheet Box" → `{ t: 'snapshot', rows, aids }`. Each bullet line is a row: when the line
  is "name: formula/fact" or "fact — remark", `f` is the first half and `note` the second;
  otherwise `f` is the whole line and `note` is `''`. `aids` are the "Memory aids" lines, word
  for word (keep their quotation marks). Drop the emoji and the shouted box title
  ("📌 SYSTEMS OF UNITS & SI UNITS — QUICK REVISION"): the part is already titled "Cheat sheet".
- A drawing → `{ t: 'fig', id }` (ids above), placed where the drawing appears in reading
  order. The figure's caption belongs to the diagram, not the text: do not repeat it as a `p`.
- Do not use `hook`, `think`, `diagram` (the old figure system) or `chip`.

## Writing HTML: the only inline markup is `<b> <i> <sup> <sub> <br>`

- `<i>` means *maths italic* in this app (it is set in the STIX serif). Use it for maths
  variables and constants exactly where the book's maths is italic: `<i>v</i>`, `<i>θ</i>`,
  `<i>g</i>`. Never for emphasis. Where the book italicises an English word for emphasis, keep
  the word and drop the italics. `<b>` keeps the book's bold.
- Upright, no `<i>`: numbers, units (`m`, `kg`, `m s<sup>−2</sup>`), dimension symbols
  (`[M L T<sup>−2</sup>]`), function names (sin, log, d in d<i>x</i>/d<i>t</i>), and words.
- Powers `<sup>`, indices `<sub>`: `<i>n</i><sub>1</sub><i>u</i><sub>1</sub>`,
  `10<sup>−10</sup>`, `<i>v</i><sup>2</sup>`.
- Characters: minus `−` (U+2212) in maths, not `-`; `×`, `·`, `±`, `≈`, `⇒`, `→`, `√`, `∫`,
  `Δ`, `°`, Greek letters as plain Unicode (θ, ω, Δ). Never the PDF's mathematical alphanumeric
  letters (𝑥, 𝜃, 𝐿 — U+1D400–U+1D7FF): the app's fonts lack them and a test rejects them.
- Fractions on one line: `a/b`, with parentheses when a side has more than one term:
  `(<i>v</i> − <i>u</i>)/<i>t</i>`, `<i>at</i><sup>2</sup>/2`, `½`. Roots: `√(<i>l</i>/<i>g</i>)`.
- Write the text as a TypeScript object literal in your file (`export const TOPICS: Topic[] =
  [ … ];`). Strings in single quotes are fine; escape `'` inside them, or use double quotes.

## Rules learned on Chapters 9 and 10

- **Run-in labels are bold.** The book sets labels like "Given:", "Assumption:", "Physical reasoning:",
